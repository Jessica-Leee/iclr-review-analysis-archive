**Review:**

This paper addresses a practical problem in domain adaptation of large language models: while continued pre-training on raw domain-specific corpora improves domain knowledge, it degrades the model's prompting ability for question answering. The authors propose transforming raw corpora into reading comprehension texts by enriching each document with task-related questions and answers. This simple, scalable method is evaluated across three domains (biomedicine, finance, law) and shows consistent improvements, with a 7B model achieving competitive performance with much larger domain-specific models like BloombergGPT-50B.

**Strengths:**
- **Clear motivation and practical insight**: The observation that raw pre-training hurts prompting ability is well-motivated and the human learning analogy (reading comprehension practice) provides intuitive justification for the proposed solution.
- **Broad empirical validation**: Evaluation across three distinct domains with multiple benchmarks demonstrates generalizability. The comparison with BloombergGPT-50B is particularly compelling, showing efficiency gains.
- **Scalability and reproducibility**: The method is applicable to any pre-training corpus and the authors promise to release code, data, and models, supporting reproducibility and adoption.
- **Cross-domain transfer**: Demonstrating that domain-specific reading comprehension texts improve general benchmarks suggests the approach has broader applicability beyond domain-specific adaptation.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited methodological novelty**: The core contribution—enriching raw text with QA pairs—is straightforward. The paper lacks technical depth in how QA pairs are generated, selected, or optimized. No ablation studies are provided to validate design choices (e.g., number of QA pairs per document, question types, answer quality).
- **Incomplete experimental details**: The abstract does not clarify how QA pairs are created (automatic generation, human annotation, or hybrid?), which is critical for reproducibility and understanding scalability claims. Comparison baselines are not specified.
- **Missing analysis**: The paper does not provide insights into *why* reading comprehension helps beyond the intuitive analogy. Analysis of what types of questions or answer patterns are most beneficial would strengthen the contribution. No error analysis or failure cases are discussed.
- **Evaluation scope**: While three domains are covered, the paper lacks analysis of how the method scales with corpus size, model size, or computational cost. The claim of "competitive performance" with BloombergGPT-50B needs more rigorous statistical comparison.

**Questions:**
- How are the QA pairs generated? Is this automatic, human-annotated, or a combination? What is the computational and annotation cost?
- What ablations were performed (e.g., number of QA pairs per document, question diversity, answer length)?
- How does performance scale with the ratio of original text to QA-enriched text?
- Are there domain-specific or task-specific patterns in which types of questions are most beneficial?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid practical contribution with clear empirical validation across multiple domains. The core idea is intuitive and the results are encouraging, particularly the efficiency gains over larger domain-specific models. However, the limited methodological novelty, lack of ablation studies, and missing technical details on QA generation prevent this from being a strong accept. The work would benefit from deeper analysis of why the approach works and more rigorous experimental design. Nevertheless, the practical impact, reproducibility commitment, and consistent improvements across domains justify acceptance.
