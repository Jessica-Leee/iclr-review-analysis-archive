**Review:**

This paper proposes TransNeXt, a vision backbone that unifies diverse attention mechanisms within a single layer through two main contributions: Aggregated Attention and Convolutional GLU. The Aggregated Attention mechanism enables each token to attend to both fine-grained local features (nearest neighbors) and coarse-grained global features via learnable tokens that diversify affinity matrix generation beyond query-key similarity. The Convolutional GLU serves as a channel mixer bridging GLU and SE mechanisms, incorporating local spatial information into channel attention. The authors claim this design eliminates the need for alternately stacking different token mixers while achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks.

**Strengths:**
- Strong empirical results across multiple scales and tasks: TransNeXt-Tiny (84.0% ImageNet at 224²) outperforms ConvNeXt-B with 69% fewer parameters; TransNeXt-Base achieves competitive results on ImageNet-A, COCO detection (57.1 mAP), and ADE20K segmentation (54.7 mIoU).
- Clear motivation for unifying local and global attention within a single layer, reducing architectural complexity compared to prior ViTs that alternate different token mixers.
- The combination of Aggregated Attention and Convolutional GLU is well-motivated, with the latter explicitly designed to enhance local modeling and robustness.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks sufficient technical detail to fully evaluate novelty. The description of how learnable tokens "interact with conventional queries and keys" and the precise mechanism for generating diverse affinity matrices remains vague. How exactly do learnable tokens differ from prior learnable components in attention?
- No ablation studies or analysis are provided in the abstract. It is unclear which components (fine-grained vs. coarse-grained attention, learnable tokens, Convolutional GLU) contribute most to performance gains, making it difficult to assess the individual merit of each contribution.
- The claim that this design "eliminates the need for alternately stacking different token mixers" is not substantiated in the abstract. A direct comparison with recent ViTs (e.g., DeiT, Swin, or other recent variants) in terms of architectural efficiency and performance trade-offs would strengthen this claim.
- Missing computational cost analysis: while parameter efficiency is highlighted (69% fewer than ConvNeXt-B), FLOPs, memory usage, and inference latency comparisons are not discussed, which are critical for practical deployment.

**Questions:**
- How do the learnable tokens in Aggregated Attention differ mechanistically from prior learnable query/key augmentations in the literature?
- What is the computational overhead of Aggregated Attention compared to standard multi-head attention?
- Can you provide ablation studies isolating the contributions of fine-grained attention, coarse-grained attention, and learnable tokens?
- How does TransNeXt compare to recent efficient ViTs (e.g., EfficientViT, MobileViT) in terms of latency and memory on mobile/edge devices?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper demonstrates strong empirical results across diverse benchmarks and proposes a reasonable unification of local and global attention mechanisms. However, the abstract provides insufficient technical detail and lacks ablation studies to fully validate the novelty and contribution of individual components. The work appears solid but would benefit from more rigorous analysis and clearer exposition of the technical innovations.
