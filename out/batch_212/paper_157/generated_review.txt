**Review:**

This paper proposes a learnable counterfactual attention (LCA) mechanism for singer identification, extending prior work (Rao et al., 2021) by replacing random counterfactual attentions with learned ones. The core idea is to introduce a counterfactual attention branch that learns to identify attention regions that are "meaningful yet not overly discriminative" (i.e., misleading), while the main branch is guided to avoid these regions and focus on truly discriminative singer-specific features. The mechanism operates only during training and thus incurs no test-time overhead. Evaluation on the artist20 benchmark reportedly shows comprehensive performance improvements.

**Strengths:** (1) The motivation is clear—learning counterfactual attentions directly from the network rather than using random ones is a reasonable and potentially more principled extension of prior work. (2) The proposed mechanism is practical: it adds a training-time auxiliary branch without affecting inference efficiency. (3) The application to fine-grained vocal recognition (singer identification) is a natural fit for attention-based methods.

**Weaknesses:** (1) The abstract provides insufficient methodological detail to assess soundness. Critically, it is unclear how the "multiple well-designed loss functions" actually enforce the desired behavior—how does the model learn to distinguish "meaningful yet misleading" attention from truly discriminative attention? This is the crux of the contribution but remains vague. (2) Evaluation is limited to a single, relatively small benchmark (artist20, from 2007). No comparison with other counterfactual attention variants, ablation studies, or results on larger/modern datasets are mentioned. (3) The paper lacks clarity on how the counterfactual branch is architecturally distinct from the main branch and how the loss functions are formally defined. (4) No discussion of related work on attention mechanisms, fine-grained recognition, or music information retrieval is provided in the abstract, making it difficult to contextualize the contribution.

The paper addresses a reasonable problem and proposes a plausible solution, but the abstract does not provide sufficient evidence of methodological rigor, comprehensive evaluation, or clear technical novelty. The core mechanism—how learnable counterfactual attention is actually learned—remains underspecified.

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. The idea has merit, but the abstract lacks sufficient technical depth, experimental breadth, and clarity to support acceptance at a top venue. Stronger empirical validation (multiple datasets, baselines, ablations) and clearer exposition of the loss functions and learning mechanism are needed.
