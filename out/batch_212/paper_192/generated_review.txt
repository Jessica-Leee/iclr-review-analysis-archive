**Review:**

This paper proves an inverse approximation theorem (Bernstein-type result) for nonlinear RNNs, showing that sequence-to-sequence relationships stably approximable by RNNs must exhibit exponential decaying memory structure. The authors extend the previously identified curse of memory from linear RNNs to the nonlinear setting and propose a reparameterization method to mitigate this fundamental architectural limitation. The theoretical contribution is grounded in approximation theory and is supported by numerical experiments.

**Strengths:**

1. **Novel theoretical contribution**: The inverse approximation theorem for nonlinear RNNs is a meaningful extension of prior results on linear RNNs. Characterizing what functions RNNs *can* approximate (via what they *must* satisfy) is a valuable perspective that clarifies fundamental limitations of the architecture.

2. **Principled approach**: The paper grounds its analysis in classical approximation theory (Bernstein-type results), providing a rigorous mathematical framework. The notion of exponential decaying memory is made precise and quantified.

3. **Practical remedy**: Beyond identifying limitations, the authors propose a reparameterization method to overcome them, bridging theory and practice.

4. **Clarity of motivation**: The paper clearly articulates the curse of memory problem and its implications for learning long-term dependencies.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Limited experimental validation**: The abstract mentions "numerical experiments" but provides no details on datasets, baselines, or quantitative comparisons. It is unclear whether the proposed reparameterization achieves practical improvements on standard benchmarks (e.g., long-range dependency tasks, language modeling).

2. **Scope and applicability unclear**: The paper does not discuss how the theoretical results apply to modern RNN variants (LSTMs, GRUs) or other sequence models (Transformers, state-space models). The relevance to current practice is therefore uncertain.

3. **Missing technical details**: The abstract does not specify the assumptions on the target function class, the stability notion used, or the precise form of the reparameterization. Without these, it is difficult to assess the tightness of the bounds or the practical utility of the method.

4. **Incomplete comparison to related work**: The paper mentions extending prior results on linear RNNs but does not discuss how the nonlinear analysis differs fundamentally or what new proof techniques are required. The novelty relative to existing inverse approximation results in other contexts is unclear.

**Questions:**

1. How does the exponential decay rate depend on the RNN architecture (width, depth, activation function)? Is the bound tight?
2. Can you provide concrete experimental results on synthetic long-memory tasks and real datasets, comparing the reparameterized RNN to standard RNNs and other baselines?
3. Do the theoretical results extend to LSTMs or GRUs, or are they specific to vanilla RNNs?
4. What is the computational overhead of the proposed reparameterization?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid theoretical contribution by extending inverse approximation theory to nonlinear RNNs and identifying fundamental memory limitations. However, the practical impact remains unclear due to limited experimental validation and missing details on the proposed method. The work would be strengthened by comprehensive experiments and clearer positioning relative to modern sequence models.
