**Review:**

EmerNeRF proposes a self-supervised approach for learning spatial-temporal representations of dynamic driving scenes using neural fields. The core contribution is a decomposition strategy that stratifies scenes into static and dynamic fields, coupled with an induced flow field that aggregates multi-frame features. Notably, this decomposition emerges purely from self-supervision without requiring ground truth object annotations or pre-trained models for segmentation or optical flow. The method additionally lifts 2D visual foundation model features into 4D space-time and addresses positional bias in Transformers to improve semantic generalization.

**Strengths:**
- **Strong empirical results**: The method achieves substantial improvements over prior work (+2.93 PSNR on static scenes, +3.70 PSNR on dynamic scenes), demonstrating practical effectiveness for sensor simulation.
- **Self-supervised decomposition**: Learning static/dynamic/flow field decomposition without ground truth annotations is a valuable contribution that enables training on general, in-the-wild data.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: The paper introduces a new 120-sequence benchmark for highly-dynamic settings and evaluates both rendering quality and downstream 3D perception tasks (e.g., occupancy prediction with 37.50% relative improvement).
- **Practical relevance**: The work addresses an important problem in autonomous driving and sensor simulation with clear applications.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited methodological novelty**: While the combination is effective, the individual components (neural fields, static/dynamic decomposition, feature aggregation) are relatively standard. The paper does not clearly articulate what is fundamentally novel beyond the specific combination and application domain.
- **Insufficient technical clarity**: The abstract lacks detail on how the decomposition "emerges" from self-supervision—what loss functions or constraints drive this emergence? How does the flow field parameterization work concretely? These details are essential for reproducibility and understanding.
- **Incomplete comparison with related work**: The abstract does not discuss how this approach relates to or improves upon recent dynamic NeRF methods (e.g., D-NeRF, Nerfies, or other dynamic scene decomposition approaches). The positioning relative to prior art is unclear.
- **Dataset and reproducibility concerns**: While the paper promises code and data release, the abstract provides limited information on dataset construction, hyperparameter choices, and training procedures that would enable independent verification.

**Questions:**
- What specific self-supervised losses or constraints enable the emergence of static/dynamic decomposition? Is there a risk of mode collapse or degenerate solutions?
- How does the induced flow field differ from or improve upon standard optical flow estimation, and why is it necessary given that optical flow is not a hard constraint?
- Can you provide more detail on the positional bias issue in Transformers and how it is addressed in the 4D lifting procedure?
- How does performance scale with scene complexity, number of frames, and motion magnitude?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper presents a well-executed application of neural fields to dynamic scene reconstruction with strong empirical results and a new benchmark. The self-supervised decomposition is a useful contribution, and the comprehensive evaluation (including downstream tasks) is commendable. However, the core methodological novelty is incremental, and the abstract lacks sufficient technical depth to fully assess the approach. The work is solid and practically valuable but does not represent a significant conceptual advance. Acceptance is warranted based on strong empirical validation and practical impact, though the contribution is somewhat incremental for a top-tier venue.
