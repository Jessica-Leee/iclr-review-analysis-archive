**Review:**

This paper addresses treatment effect estimation under limited overlap by proposing a method to simultaneously recover identifiable balanced prognostic scores and balancing scores. The key contribution is demonstrating that, under weak conditions, one can relax the overlap requirement by working in a latent space, thereby enabling generalization beyond overlapped regions and handling out-of-distribution treatments. The approach is claimed to be adaptable to both binary and structured treatment settings.

**Strengths:**

1. **Important problem**: Limited overlap is a well-recognized challenge in causal inference, and methods that relax this assumption have significant practical value.
2. **Theoretical motivation**: The paper claims to establish identifiability of balanced prognostic scores and balancing scores under weak conditions, which is a meaningful theoretical contribution if rigorously developed.
3. **Generality**: The stated adaptability to multiple treatment settings (binary and structured) suggests broad applicability.
4. **Empirical validation**: The abstract indicates state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Insufficient technical detail in abstract**: The abstract does not specify what the "weak conditions" are, how identifiability is established, or what the latent space construction entails. This makes it impossible to assess the technical soundness or novelty of the theoretical contributions.
2. **Missing methodological clarity**: The paper does not describe the actual algorithm or optimization procedure. How are the balanced prognostic scores and balancing scores jointly recovered? What is the objective function or loss?
3. **Vague claims about out-of-distribution treatments**: The statement that the method "handles out-of-distribution treatments with no overlap" is strong but unexplained. Without formal justification, this raises questions about identifiability and generalization guarantees.
4. **Limited empirical context**: The abstract mentions "different benchmarks" and "state-of-the-art performance" but provides no specifics—no dataset names, baselines, or quantitative results are given.

**Questions:**

1. What are the "weak conditions" under which identifiability holds? Are they weaker than standard overlap assumptions?
2. How is the latent space constructed, and what ensures that overlap is achieved in this space?
3. Can you provide the formal identifiability proof or at least a sketch of the key argument?
4. What is the concrete algorithm for jointly learning balanced prognostic and balancing scores?
5. How does performance compare quantitatively to existing methods on standard benchmarks (e.g., IHDP, Twins)?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles an important problem and claims meaningful theoretical and empirical contributions. However, the abstract lacks sufficient technical detail to evaluate the core claims. The identifiability conditions, the mechanism for relaxing overlap, and the algorithmic approach are all underspecified. While the motivation is sound, the presentation is too vague for a venue like ICLR, where rigor and clarity are essential. A full paper with detailed theory, clear algorithms, and comprehensive experiments would be needed to assess this work properly.
