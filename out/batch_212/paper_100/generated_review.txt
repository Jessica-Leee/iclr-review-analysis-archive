**Review:**

This paper proposes a general formulation for independent policy optimization in fully decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) through f-divergence policy optimization, and introduces TVPO, a novel algorithm with convergence guarantees. The authors argue that while independent learning methods like independent Q-learning and independent PPO are practical, they lack theoretical support. The f-divergence formulation is presented as a unifying framework, and TVPO is derived from this framework with claimed convergence guarantees. Empirically, TVPO is evaluated on three cooperative MARL benchmarks against state-of-the-art fully decentralized methods.

**Strengths:**
- The paper addresses an important gap: providing theoretical convergence guarantees for independent learning in decentralized MARL, which has historically lacked formal analysis.
- The f-divergence policy optimization formulation offers a potentially unifying perspective on independent learning algorithms, connecting disparate existing methods.
- Empirical validation on multiple popular cooperative MARL benchmarks demonstrates practical improvements over baselines.
- The focus on fully decentralized learning (without a central coordinator) is practically relevant and challenging.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides insufficient detail about the f-divergence formulation itself—what specific f-divergences are considered, how they relate to existing policy gradient methods, and what the "limitation" of the formulation is remain unclear. This limits assessment of the theoretical contribution's novelty and scope.
- The convergence guarantees for TVPO are mentioned but not characterized in the abstract (e.g., convergence rate, assumptions required, comparison to existing convergence results). Without this detail, the strength of the theoretical contribution cannot be evaluated.
- The paper does not clarify how TVPO differs algorithmically from independent PPO or other independent methods beyond the theoretical framework, making it difficult to assess whether improvements are due to the theory or algorithmic novelty.
- Limited discussion of when and why the f-divergence formulation is preferable to existing policy optimization approaches, and what practical insights the theory provides for algorithm design.

**Questions:**
- Can you provide the convergence rate and key assumptions for TVPO's convergence guarantee? How does this compare to convergence results for centralized or other decentralized MARL methods?
- Which f-divergences are used in TVPO, and how do they relate to standard policy gradient objectives (e.g., KL divergence in PPO)?
- What is the specific limitation of the f-divergence formulation mentioned in the abstract, and does TVPO overcome it?
- How does TVPO's computational and communication complexity scale with the number of agents compared to baselines?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid contribution by providing a theoretical framework and convergence guarantees for independent learning in decentralized MARL, an area that has lacked formal analysis. The empirical results on standard benchmarks are encouraging. However, the abstract is vague about the theoretical details and the practical algorithmic novelty, which limits confidence in the significance of the contribution. A full paper would likely clarify these points and strengthen the assessment.
