**Review:**

This paper proposes ChatDrug, a framework for drug editing that combines conversational LLMs with a retrieval and domain feedback module to systematically explore molecular, peptide, and protein editing tasks. The approach jointly leverages prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, and domain-specific feedback to guide the LLM toward generating valid and chemically sound drug modifications. The authors evaluate ChatDrug on 39 drug editing tasks and provide case studies demonstrating the model's ability to identify key substructures and generate diverse suggestions with domain-specific explanations.

**Strengths:**
- **Timely and underexplored problem**: Drug editing is a critical but understudied application of conversational LLMs in drug discovery, filling a gap beyond existing work on retrosynthesis and chemical reactions.
- **Comprehensive scope**: The evaluation spans three distinct molecular types (small molecules, peptides, proteins) across 39 tasks, suggesting broad applicability.
- **Interpretability focus**: The emphasis on domain-specific explanations and case studies (10 examples provided) enhances practical utility for domain experts making informed decisions.
- **Modular design**: The three-module architecture (prompt, retrieval and domain feedback, conversation) is conceptually clear and allows for systematic investigation.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited methodological novelty**: The paper appears to combine existing techniques (prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, LLM conversation) without introducing novel algorithmic or architectural contributions. The specific design choices for the retrieval and domain feedback module are not detailed in the abstract.
- **Insufficient evaluation details**: The abstract provides no quantitative metrics, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance testing. Claims of "best performance on all 39 tasks" lack supporting numbers, error bars, or comparison to alternative approaches (e.g., standard LLM prompting, other retrieval methods).
- **Vague technical description**: Key components—particularly the "retrieval and domain feedback module"—are not sufficiently described. How domain feedback is incorporated, what retrieval corpus is used, and how these components interact remain unclear.
- **Limited validation of claims**: While 10 case studies are mentioned, the abstract does not specify whether these are cherry-picked examples or representative samples, nor does it provide quantitative evidence that the generated suggestions are "valid" or "diverse" beyond qualitative inspection.

**Questions:**
- What are the specific quantitative metrics used to evaluate drug editing quality, and how does ChatDrug compare numerically to baseline LLM approaches?
- How is the retrieval corpus constructed, and what domain knowledge sources inform the feedback module?
- Can the authors provide inter-rater agreement or validation metrics for the case studies to substantiate claims of correctness?
- How sensitive is the framework to prompt design, and what ablation studies were conducted?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a reasonable framework, but the contribution is primarily engineering-focused (combining existing techniques) rather than methodologically novel. The evaluation is insufficiently detailed in the abstract to assess the strength of empirical claims. For a venue like ICLR, the work would benefit from deeper technical innovation, rigorous quantitative evaluation with baselines, and clearer exposition of the retrieval and domain feedback mechanisms.
