**Review:**

This paper proposes a self-supervised evaluation framework for LLMs that measures model sensitivity or invariance to input transformations, rather than relying on human-curated labeled datasets. The approach is motivated by practical deployment concerns—evaluating models on realistic, in-the-wild data without the pitfalls of data leakage or narrow distribution sampling. The authors demonstrate the framework across multiple tasks: closed-book knowledge, toxicity, long-range context dependence, and robustness to grammatical and tokenization errors. They report strong correlations between self-sensitivity metrics and human-supervised evaluations on comparable benchmarks.

**Strengths:**
- **Practical motivation and scope:** The paper addresses a genuine problem in LLM evaluation—the limitations of small, curated benchmarks and the risk of train-test contamination. The ability to evaluate on arbitrary user data or live deployment streams is valuable.
- **Diverse task coverage:** Demonstrating the framework across multiple evaluation dimensions (toxicity, knowledge, context dependence, robustness) shows generality and suggests the approach is not narrowly tailored.
- **Empirical validation:** Reporting correlations with human-labeled benchmarks provides evidence that self-sensitivity metrics capture meaningful model behavior, grounding the self-supervised approach in established evaluation standards.
- **Complementary framing:** The paper positions self-supervised evaluation as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, human-labeled evaluation, which is a reasonable and honest framing.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited methodological novelty:** The core idea—measuring model robustness via input transformations—is not new. The paper does not clearly articulate what is novel beyond applying this well-established technique to LLM evaluation. The specific transformations used (grammatical perturbations, tokenization errors, etc.) appear straightforward and lack technical depth.
- **Insufficient detail on methodology:** The abstract provides little concrete information about how transformations are designed, applied, or validated. For instance, how are "grammatical structure" transformations defined? How sensitive are results to transformation choice? Without methodological clarity, reproducibility and generalizability are unclear.
- **Correlation is not validation:** While correlations with human benchmarks are reported, the abstract does not specify the strength of these correlations, the number of tasks evaluated, or whether they are statistically significant. Moderate correlations could indicate the self-supervised metrics capture only partial signal.
- **Missing critical analysis:** The paper does not discuss failure modes, limitations of the self-sensitivity paradigm, or cases where it might diverge from human judgment. For example, when might a model be invariant to a transformation in a way that is actually desirable? How do we distinguish signal from noise?

**Questions:**
- What is the range and statistical significance of reported correlations with human-supervised benchmarks?
- How are input transformations designed and validated? Are they task-specific or general?
- Can you provide concrete examples of cases where self-sensitivity metrics disagree with human evaluation, and how these are interpreted?
- How does computational cost compare to traditional evaluation, especially for large-scale deployment monitoring?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a practical and timely problem in LLM evaluation and demonstrates a working framework with empirical grounding in human benchmarks. However, the methodological contribution is incremental—applying known robustness-testing ideas to LLM evaluation—and the presentation lacks sufficient technical depth to fully assess the approach's rigor and generalizability. The work is useful as a complementary evaluation tool but does not represent a significant methodological advance. It merits publication as a solid workshop or applications paper, though it sits at the margin for a top-tier venue.
