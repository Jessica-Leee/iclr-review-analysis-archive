**Review:**

This paper introduces the interaction tensor as a tool for empirically analyzing how data, models, and features interact in deep learning. The authors use this framework to derive closed-form expressions for expected accuracy of a single hypothesis and agreement between pairs of hypotheses, and they connect their framework to the recently discovered Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE). The work aims to provide theoretical insight into feature learning by explaining empirical phenomena and constructing data distributions that violate GDE.

**Strengths:**

- The interaction tensor is a novel empirical tool that offers a fresh perspective on understanding feature learning and the relationship between data and model behavior across different random seeds.
- The paper makes concrete theoretical contributions by deriving closed-form expressions for accuracy and agreement, and by providing explicit constructions of data distributions that break GDE—a non-trivial result that challenges recent claims.
- The work addresses an important gap in deep learning theory: understanding the role of learned features, which is fundamental to the field yet theoretically underdeveloped.
- The connection to GDE grounds the work in recent empirical findings and demonstrates practical relevance.

**Weaknesses:**

- The abstract provides limited detail on the actual form of the interaction tensor, its construction, and the assumptions underlying the closed-form derivations. Without seeing the full paper, it is difficult to assess whether the framework is sufficiently general or relies on restrictive assumptions.
- The empirical validation and scope of experiments are not described in the abstract. It remains unclear on which datasets, architectures, and settings the proposed framework has been tested, and how well the theoretical predictions match empirical observations in practice.
- The paper claims to "explain empirically observed phenomena" but the abstract does not specify which phenomena beyond GDE are explained, or provide quantitative evidence of explanatory power.
- The construction of data distributions breaking GDE is mentioned but not detailed; without understanding the nature of these constructions (e.g., are they natural or pathological?), it is hard to assess the significance of this result.

**Questions:**

- What are the key assumptions of the proposed conceptual framework, and how restrictive are they in practice?
- How does the interaction tensor scale with model size and data dimensionality, and what are the computational costs of computing it?
- Can you provide concrete examples of the closed-form expressions for accuracy and agreement, and how well they predict empirical values on standard benchmarks?
- What is the nature of the data distributions that break GDE—are they realistic or primarily of theoretical interest?

**Overall score:**

6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles an important theoretical question and proposes a novel tool with concrete contributions (closed-form expressions, GDE violations). However, the abstract lacks sufficient detail on methodology, assumptions, and empirical validation to fully assess the work's rigor and impact. The ideas appear sound and potentially valuable, but the presentation in the abstract is too sparse to confidently recommend acceptance without seeing the full paper.
