**Review:**

This paper presents a comprehensive investigation of noise scheduling in diffusion-based generative models, combining theoretical analysis with practical improvements. The authors introduce weighted signal-noise-ratio (WSNR) as a unified metric for noise representation across RGB and latent spaces, propose WSNR-Equivalent training noise schedules, and analyze the reverse sampling process through an ODE framework to derive insights about optimal denoisers and data-driven sampling schedules. They further study the relationship between evaluation points and generation quality, proposing an adaptive scheme for selecting numerical methods. The approach requires no additional training and achieves modest improvements on CIFAR-10 (1.92→1.89 FID) and FFHQ-64 (2.45→2.25 FID) using 35 network evaluations.

**Strengths:**
- The paper addresses an important and practical problem: understanding and optimizing noise schedules in diffusion models, which directly impacts both training efficiency and sampling quality.
- The introduction of WSNR as a unified metric for noise representation across different spaces (RGB and latent) is a useful contribution that could simplify hyperparameter tuning and model scaling.
- The ODE-based analysis of the reverse sampling process provides theoretical grounding for understanding optimal denoisers and sampling schedules, bridging theory and practice.
- The proposed approach is practical—requiring no retraining—making it immediately applicable to existing pre-trained models.

**Weaknesses:**
- The empirical improvements, while consistent, are relatively modest (1–2% FID reduction). The paper does not clearly establish whether these gains are statistically significant or practically meaningful, nor does it provide error bars or multiple runs.
- The paper lacks comprehensive experimental validation. Results are shown only on two relatively small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10 and FFHQ-64); evaluation on larger, more challenging benchmarks (ImageNet, higher resolutions) would strengthen claims about generalizability.
- The presentation of the ODE framework and its connection to the practical improvements could be clearer. The abstract mentions "optimal denoiser" and "data-driven sampling noise schedules" but does not explain how these concepts directly lead to the proposed adaptive scheme or the WSNR-Equivalent schedules.
- Missing ablation studies and comparisons: the paper does not isolate the contributions of WSNR-Equivalent training schedules versus the adaptive ODE solver scheme, nor does it compare against other recent noise schedule optimization methods.

**Questions:**
- How sensitive are the improvements to the choice of evaluation points? Can you provide ablation studies showing FID as a function of the number of evaluations?
- Have you tested the approach on higher-resolution images and larger datasets? Do the improvements scale?
- Can you clarify the relationship between the ODE analysis (optimal denoiser, data-driven schedules) and the practical WSNR metric and adaptive scheme?
- How do the proposed methods compare quantitatively to other recent noise schedule optimization or sampling acceleration techniques?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes solid contributions to understanding and improving noise schedules in diffusion models through both theory (ODE analysis, WSNR metric) and practice (adaptive sampling). However, the empirical gains are modest and limited to small-scale datasets, and the presentation could better connect the theoretical insights to the practical improvements. The work is technically sound and addresses a relevant problem, but lacks the depth of experimental validation and clarity of exposition expected for a strong accept.
