**Review:**

This paper introduces WI3D, a framework for class-incremental 3D object detection that learns novel categories from cost-effective 2D visual prompts rather than full 3D annotations. The approach combines three key components: (i) a class-agnostic pseudo label refinement module to generate high-quality 3D pseudo labels from 2D prompts, (ii) cross-modal knowledge transfer for learning novel class representations, and (iii) reweighting knowledge distillation to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of base classes. The work addresses a practical and important problem—reducing annotation cost in incremental 3D detection—and demonstrates improvements over baselines on SUN-RGBD and ScanNet datasets.

**Strengths:**
- **Practical motivation**: The shift from expensive 3D annotations to 2D visual prompts for incremental learning is well-motivated and reduces labeling burden, addressing a real bottleneck in 3D detection deployment.
- **Comprehensive framework**: The three-component design (pseudo label refinement, cross-modal transfer, reweighting distillation) is coherent and targets the key challenges of weak supervision and catastrophic forgetting simultaneously.
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments on two standard benchmarks (SUN-RGBD, ScanNet) under multiple incremental settings provide reasonable evidence of the method's effectiveness.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited technical novelty**: While the combination is sensible, the individual components (pseudo labeling, knowledge distillation, cross-modal learning) are well-established techniques. The paper does not clearly articulate what is novel beyond their application to this specific setting.
- **Insufficient methodological detail**: The abstract lacks specifics on how the class-agnostic pseudo label refinement works, what the cross-modal knowledge transfer mechanism is, or how reweighting distillation differs from standard approaches. Without these details, reproducibility and technical assessment are hindered.
- **Incomplete experimental analysis**: The abstract mentions "extensive experiments" but provides no quantitative results, ablation studies, or comparisons to related work. It is unclear how much each component contributes or how the method compares to other incremental learning or weakly-supervised 3D detection approaches.
- **Unclear scope of "visual prompts"**: The definition of 2D visual prompts is vague. Are these bounding boxes, segmentation masks, or something else? The relationship between 2D prompts and 3D pseudo label generation needs clarification.

**Questions:**
- How exactly does the class-agnostic pseudo label refinement module leverage 2D prompts to generate 3D labels? What is the geometric or learning mechanism?
- What is the specific form of cross-modal knowledge transfer, and how does it differ from standard multi-modal fusion?
- Can you provide quantitative results (e.g., mAP improvements, forgetting metrics) on the two datasets to substantiate the claims?
- How does WI3D compare to other weakly-supervised or semi-supervised 3D detection methods?
