**Review:**

This paper investigates margin maximization in gradient-based learning on linearly separable data and proposes Progressive Rescaling Gradient Descent (PRGD), which claims to achieve exponential-rate margin maximization. The key contribution is a theoretical analysis of the velocity field associated with normalized gradients and the identification of conditions under which standard gradient descent (GD) and normalized gradient descent (NGD) fail to maximize margin efficiently. The authors then propose PRGD as a remedy and provide experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets.

**Strengths:**
- The theoretical focus on margin maximization dynamics and the identification of failure modes for existing algorithms (GD, NGD) is interesting and potentially valuable for understanding implicit bias.
- The claimed exponential rate for margin maximization (versus polynomial for existing methods) would be a significant improvement if rigorously established.
- The paper attempts validation across synthetic, real-world, and deep neural network settings, showing breadth of evaluation.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract and summary lack sufficient detail on the mechanism of PRGD. What precisely is "progressive norm rescaling"? How does it differ fundamentally from existing rescaling or normalization schemes? Without clarity on the algorithmic innovation, the contribution is hard to assess.
- The conditions under which GD and NGD "provably fail" are stated as "mild" but are not specified in the abstract. This vagueness raises concerns: are these conditions realistic or pathological? How restrictive are they?
- The extension to linearly non-separable data and deep neural networks is mentioned only as showing "promise," suggesting these results may be preliminary or empirical rather than theoretical. The gap between the linear separable theory and practical deep learning applications is not bridged clearly.
- The paper does not discuss computational cost or practical overhead of PRGD relative to standard methods, which is important for practitioners.

**Questions:**
- Can you provide a precise, intuitive description of the progressive norm rescaling mechanism in the main text?
- What are the explicit "mild conditions" under which GD and NGD fail, and how common are they in practice?
- Do the theoretical guarantees for PRGD apply to the non-separable and deep learning settings, or are those results purely empirical?
- What is the per-iteration computational cost of PRGD compared to GD/NGD?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a theoretically interesting question (margin maximization dynamics) and proposes a method with claimed exponential improvements. However, the presentation is somewhat opaque regarding the core algorithmic contribution, the conditions for failure of baselines are not clearly stated, and the practical applicability beyond the linear separable setting remains unclear. With revisions to clarify the mechanism of PRGD, specify the failure conditions, and better connect theory to practice, this could be a solid contribution. As presented, it is marginally acceptable but would benefit from greater clarity and completeness.
