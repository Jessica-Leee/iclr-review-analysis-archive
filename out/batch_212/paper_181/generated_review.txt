**Review:**

This paper proposes using approximate empirical neural tangent kernels (eNTK) as surrogate models for explaining neural network predictions, combining recent trends in surrogate-based explainability and kernel-based data attribution. The authors introduce novel approximations of eNTK—including two random projection variants—that reduce computational cost while maintaining correlation with the underlying network. They analyze how well kernel machines using these approximate eNTK functions serve as faithful surrogates and identify trace NTK as the most consistent performer.

**Strengths:**

1. **Well-motivated problem:** The paper addresses a genuine computational bottleneck in eNTK analysis by proposing practical approximations with tunable time-memory tradeoffs via random projection variants. This is a pragmatic contribution to making NTK-based explanations scalable.

2. **Clear methodological contribution:** The introduction of approximate eNTK variants (particularly trace NTK) with explicit analysis of their correlation to the original network is concrete and measurable. The paper combines two established trends (surrogate modeling and kernel-based attribution) in a coherent way.

3. **Empirical validation:** The authors provide empirical evidence that their approximate eNTK surrogates correlate well with neural network behavior, supporting the practical utility of the approach.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Limited novelty in individual components:** While the combination is sensible, the paper primarily adapts existing concepts (eNTK, random projections, kernel surrogates) rather than introducing fundamentally new theory or methodology. The theoretical analysis of approximation quality is not deeply developed in the abstract.

2. **Incomplete evaluation details:** The abstract lacks specifics on experimental scope—which datasets, architectures, and baselines are used? How does trace NTK compare quantitatively to other surrogate approaches (e.g., LIME, SHAP, or other kernel-based methods)? Without these details, it is difficult to assess the practical impact.

3. **Unclear explanation quality:** The paper focuses on surrogate fidelity but does not clearly articulate how well these surrogates actually explain individual predictions or enable actionable insights. The connection between kernel machine correlation and explanation faithfulness could be made more explicit.

4. **Missing ablation and analysis:** The paper would benefit from ablation studies on the random projection variants, analysis of when trace NTK outperforms alternatives, and discussion of failure cases or limitations.

**Questions:**

- How do the proposed approximate eNTK surrogates compare quantitatively to standard explainability methods (LIME, SHAP, attention-based methods) on standard benchmarks?
- What is the empirical scaling behavior of the random projection variants as network size and dataset size increase?
- Can you provide concrete examples where the trace NTK surrogate provides more faithful explanations than existing methods?
- How sensitive are the results to the choice of random projection dimension?

**Overall score:**

6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid, practical contribution by proposing computationally efficient approximations to eNTK for surrogate-based explanations. The combination of ideas is sensible and the empirical validation supports the approach. However, the novelty is somewhat incremental (adapting existing techniques rather than introducing new theory), and the evaluation lacks depth in comparing to competing explanation methods and demonstrating real-world utility. The work is technically sound and addresses a real problem, but falls short of being a strong accept due to limited scope and incomplete experimental validation as presented in the abstract.
