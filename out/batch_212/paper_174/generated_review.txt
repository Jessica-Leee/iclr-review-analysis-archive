**Review:**

This paper proposes a theoretical framework for understanding adversarial attacks on Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) and introduces a novel attack method that exploits a unified target to guide adversarial perturbations through both the forward and reverse processes of LDMs. The authors claim their approach addresses an "offset problem" in existing optimization-based attacks and demonstrate improved generalization across different few-shot generation pipelines. While the motivation is clear—protecting personal images from unauthorized use in generative models—the paper lacks sufficient detail in the abstract to fully evaluate the theoretical contributions and the nature of the proposed unified target mechanism.

**Strengths:**
- Addresses a timely and important problem: adversarial robustness of increasingly powerful generative models used in few-shot generation.
- Claims to provide a theoretical framework for understanding adversarial attacks on LDMs, moving beyond purely empirical approaches.
- Proposes a method that reportedly generalizes across multiple state-of-the-art few-shot generation pipelines, suggesting broader applicability.
- Identifies and attempts to resolve a concrete technical issue (the "offset problem") in existing attack optimization.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides no concrete details about the theoretical framework itself—what mathematical formulation, assumptions, or insights does it provide? Without specifics, the theoretical contribution is difficult to assess.
- The "unified target" mechanism is mentioned but not explained; it is unclear how this differs fundamentally from existing attack objectives or why it resolves the offset problem.
- No quantitative results, baselines, or experimental details are provided in the abstract. Claims of outperformance and generalization cannot be verified without seeing actual numbers, datasets, or comparisons.
- The paper does not discuss potential limitations, failure cases, or the computational cost of the proposed method relative to existing attacks.

**Questions:**
- What is the mathematical form of the theoretical framework, and what are its key assumptions about the LDM architecture or the attack setting?
- How is the "unified target" defined, and what is the offset problem it solves in concrete terms?
- What are the quantitative improvements over existing methods (e.g., attack success rate, perturbation magnitude, computational efficiency)?
- How does the method perform on defenses or certified robustness mechanisms, if any exist for LDMs?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a relevant problem and hints at both theoretical and empirical contributions, but the abstract is too vague to assess the actual novelty and rigor of the work. The lack of concrete details about the theoretical framework, the proposed method's mechanism, and quantitative experimental results makes it impossible to evaluate whether the contributions are substantial enough for a top-tier venue. A significantly more detailed abstract or full paper would be needed to properly assess this work.
