**Review:**

This paper proposes Simplicial SMOTE, a generalization of the classical SMOTE oversampling method that leverages topological data analysis. Rather than sampling synthetic points as convex combinations of a data point and a single k-nearest neighbor (sampling from edges), the method samples from simplices of a geometric neighborhood simplicial complex, using barycentric coordinates with respect to simplices spanned by multiple nearby points. The authors evaluate their approach on 23 benchmark datasets and demonstrate improvements over SMOTE and several extensions, including a simplicial variant of Borderline SMOTE.

**Strengths:**
- The core idea is intuitive and well-motivated: extending SMOTE's geometric sampling from edges to higher-dimensional simplices is a natural generalization with clear topological grounding.
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation on 23 benchmark datasets with comparisons to multiple SMOTE variants provides reasonable evidence of practical utility.
- The method is modular and can be integrated into existing SMOTE extensions (demonstrated with Borderline SMOTE), suggesting broader applicability.
- The use of tools from topological data analysis is a fresh perspective on a classical problem.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract and summary lack crucial methodological details: How are simplices selected or constructed? What is the computational complexity compared to SMOTE? How sensitive is the method to the choice of simplex dimension or neighborhood parameters?
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals are reported for the benchmark comparisons, making it unclear whether improvements are meaningful or within noise margins.
- The paper does not discuss theoretical justification for why sampling from higher-dimensional simplices should improve generalization or class balance. The connection between topological properties and oversampling quality remains implicit.
- Missing ablation studies: What is the contribution of simplex dimension? How does performance vary with different simplex construction strategies? These are essential for understanding the method's behavior.
- Limited discussion of when Simplicial SMOTE might fail or underperform, and no analysis of computational overhead, which could be significant for high-dimensional simplicial complexes.

**Questions:**
- How is the simplicial complex constructed in practice, and what is its computational cost relative to SMOTE?
- Are the improvements on the 23 datasets statistically significant? What are the effect sizes?
- How does performance scale with dataset dimensionality and imbalance ratio?
- Can you provide intuition or theoretical analysis for why sampling from simplices improves upon edge-based sampling?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper presents a reasonable and intuitive extension of SMOTE with empirical validation on multiple datasets. However, the presentation lacks important methodological details, statistical rigor in comparisons, and theoretical grounding. The contribution is incremental but solid—a natural generalization of an established method with demonstrated practical improvements. The work would benefit from deeper analysis of when and why the method works, but the core idea and empirical results are sufficient for publication at a venue like ICLR.
