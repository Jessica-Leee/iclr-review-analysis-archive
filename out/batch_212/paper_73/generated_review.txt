**Review:**

This paper investigates how calibration can be transferred from teacher to student classifiers via knowledge distillation (KD). The authors provide theoretical analysis on a special class of linear teacher-student classifiers, showing that calibrated teachers can distill calibrated students, and demonstrating that calibration transfer can occur even from lower to higher capacity models (reverse-KD). A key empirical finding is that teachers trained with dynamic label smoothing produce better-calibrated students. The proposed KD-based calibration framework achieves strong empirical results, reporting ECE of 0.98 on CIFAR-100 with WRN-40-1, substantially outperforming recent methods like Adafocal (7.61) and CPC (2.1).

**Strengths:**

1. **Novel perspective on calibration transfer**: The paper addresses an understudied but practically important question—whether calibration can be transferred via KD—and provides both theoretical insights (on linear classifiers) and empirical validation across architectures.

2. **Strong empirical results**: The reported ECE improvements are substantial and consistent across multiple architectures (WRN-40-1, MobileNetv2), with the proposed method achieving state-of-the-art calibration performance compared to Adafocal and CPC.

3. **Interesting reverse-KD finding**: The observation that calibration can transfer from lower to higher capacity teachers is counterintuitive and adds depth to understanding KD beyond traditional accuracy-focused distillation.

4. **Practical insight on training regime**: The finding that dynamic label smoothing in teacher training improves student calibration is actionable and well-motivated.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Limited theoretical scope**: The theoretical analysis is restricted to linear classifiers, which is a significant simplification. The paper does not clearly establish how insights from the linear case transfer to the deep neural networks used in experiments, limiting the explanatory power of the theory.

2. **Incomplete experimental details and ablations**: The abstract does not provide sufficient detail on the experimental setup, hyperparameter choices, or ablation studies. For instance, how sensitive are results to the choice of dynamic label smoothing parameters? How does the method compare to simpler baselines (e.g., applying label smoothing directly to the student)?

3. **Missing comparisons and analysis**: The paper does not discuss or compare against other recent calibration methods beyond Adafocal and CPC. Additionally, the mechanism by which dynamic label smoothing improves calibration transfer is not thoroughly analyzed—is this a general property of KD or specific to the proposed setup?

4. **Presentation clarity**: The abstract jumps between theoretical claims (linear classifiers) and empirical results (deep networks) without clearly delineating the scope of each contribution. The connection between theory and practice remains somewhat opaque.

**Questions:**

1. How do the theoretical insights from linear classifiers inform the design or understanding of the deep network experiments? Can you provide intuition for why the linear analysis is relevant?

2. What is the computational overhead of the proposed KD-based calibration compared to post-hoc methods like temperature scaling?

3. Have you tested the method on out-of-distribution or domain-shift scenarios, which are critical for assessing calibration in practice?

4. Can you provide ablation studies isolating the contribution of dynamic label smoothing versus the KD framework itself?

---

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid contribution by systematically studying calibration transfer via KD, provides both theory (albeit limited to linear models) and strong empirical results. The reverse-KD finding and the practical insight about dynamic label smoothing are valuable. However, the gap between linear theory and deep network practice, combined with incomplete experimental details and limited ablation studies, prevents this from being a strong accept. The empirical results are impressive enough to merit publication, but the paper would benefit from deeper analysis of the mechanisms at play.
