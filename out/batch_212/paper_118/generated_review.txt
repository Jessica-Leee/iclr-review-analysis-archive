**Review:**

This paper addresses memory constraints in Spiking Neural Network (SNN) training by proposing a reversible SNN node design and a specialized backpropagation algorithm. The authors claim a 58.65× reduction in memory usage compared to standard SNN nodes and a 23.8% reduction in training time relative to existing reversible layer architectures. The core idea of leveraging reversibility to reduce spike/event storage during training is conceptually sound and well-motivated for resource-constrained SNN applications.

However, the paper suffers from significant presentation and validation issues that prevent proper assessment. The abstract provides only high-level claims without describing the reversible SNN node design, the specific backpropagation algorithm, or the datasets and baselines used for evaluation. Critically, no experimental results, comparisons, or ablation studies are provided in the abstract—only claimed speedups and memory reductions without supporting evidence. The paper does not clarify how reversibility is achieved for SNNs (which involve discrete spike events), whether the approach applies to all SNN architectures, or how accuracy is maintained under the claimed memory constraints. Without access to the full paper's methodology, experimental setup, and results, it is impossible to verify the claimed improvements or assess their significance relative to prior work on memory-efficient SNN training.

The contribution, if validated, could be valuable for practical SNN deployment. However, the current submission lacks the rigor and completeness expected for a top-tier venue. The abstract reads more as a technical report summary than a research contribution, and the absence of any experimental validation or methodological detail in the provided text raises concerns about the maturity and reproducibility of the work.
