**Review:**

This paper proposes Adversarial Instance Attacks, a black-box framework for attacking Human-Object Interaction (HOI) detection models by generating adversarial instances that disrupt interaction recognition without necessarily fooling object detection. The approach introduces three key components: an Interaction Area Sampling module to identify vulnerable anchor regions, an Object Category Search module that leverages a co-occurrence knowledge graph to find obfuscating object categories, and a perturbation generation mechanism that places adversarial instances in vulnerable areas to achieve high co-occurrence obfuscation toward target interactions.

**Strengths:**
- **Novel problem formulation**: The paper addresses an underexplored attack surface—HOI detection—which is more complex than standard object detection and represents a meaningful extension of adversarial robustness evaluation.
- **Structured methodology**: The three-module design (area sampling, category search via knowledge graph, perturbation generation) is well-motivated and appears technically sound for the stated goal of attacking interactions without disrupting object detection.
- **Empirical improvements**: The reported gains of at least +10.36% over existing methods suggest the approach is effective in practice.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Insufficient methodological detail**: The abstract provides only high-level descriptions of the three modules. Critical details are missing: How exactly does the Interaction Area Sampling identify vulnerable anchors? What is the precise formulation of the co-occurrence obfuscation score? How are perturbations generated and optimized? Without these specifics, reproducibility and technical assessment are severely limited.
- **Limited experimental context**: The abstract mentions "extensive experiments" and "multiple models" but provides no concrete details—no dataset names, baseline methods, model architectures, or quantitative comparisons beyond the single +10.36% figure. This makes it impossible to assess the significance of the results or the validity of the claims.
- **Unclear novelty boundaries**: While attacking HOI is novel, the paper does not clearly articulate what is fundamentally new beyond applying existing adversarial attack ideas to a new task. The use of knowledge graphs and area sampling, while sensible, lacks justification for why these specific design choices are necessary or superior.
- **Presentation quality**: The abstract contains a formatting error ("improvement of at least +10.36%}") and lacks clarity on key concepts (e.g., what "co-occurrence obfuscation" means precisely, how the black-box constraint is enforced).

**Questions:**
- Can you provide the full experimental setup, including datasets (e.g., HICO-DET, V-COCO), baseline methods, and attacked models?
- How is the co-occurrence obfuscation score formally defined, and how does it relate to standard adversarial loss functions?
- What is the computational cost of the knowledge graph construction and category search compared to simpler baseline attacks?
- How sensitive is the method to hyperparameters, and how are they selected?
