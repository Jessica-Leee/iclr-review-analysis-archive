**Review:**

This paper proposes a personalized prompt rewriting approach for text-to-image generation, framing large pretrained models as search engines and adapting query rewriting techniques to enhance user prompts. The authors introduce a new large-scale dataset with over 300k prompts from 3115 users and develop a rewriting model that leverages historical user interactions to improve prompt expressiveness and alignment with intended visual outputs. The work addresses a genuine usability challenge—helping users articulate their visual preferences in ways that generative models can effectively interpret.

**Strengths:**
- Addresses a practical and important problem: many users struggle to craft effective prompts for text-to-image systems, and personalization based on user history is a natural and valuable direction.
- Introduces a substantial new dataset (300k+ prompts from 3115 users) that could be valuable for future research in personalized generation.
- The core idea of applying search engine techniques (query rewriting) to generative models is intuitive and well-motivated.
- Proposes both an offline evaluation method and online testing, suggesting empirical validation of the approach.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks critical technical details: the paper does not specify the architecture of the rewriting model, the training procedure, or how historical user interactions are encoded and leveraged. It is unclear whether the approach uses retrieval-augmented methods, fine-tuning, or in-context learning.
- No baseline comparisons or quantitative results are provided in the abstract. Phrases like "superiority of our methods over baseline approaches" are unsupported without concrete numbers, metrics, or named baselines.
- The "offline evaluation method" is mentioned but not described. Without knowing what this metric measures or how it relates to user satisfaction, it is difficult to assess the validity of the claims.
- Limited discussion of generalization: it is unclear whether the rewriting model generalizes to new users, new prompts, or different text-to-image systems, or whether it requires extensive per-user data.

**Questions:**
- How does the rewriting model incorporate user history? Are embeddings of past prompts and generated images used, or is a retrieval mechanism employed?
- What are the specific baselines, and what quantitative improvements (e.g., in CLIP scores, user preference ratings, or other metrics) does the method achieve?
- How much historical interaction data is required per user for the rewriting model to be effective?
- Does the approach generalize to users with limited interaction history, and how does performance degrade with sparse user data?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. While the problem is well-motivated and the dataset contribution is valuable, the abstract provides insufficient technical and empirical detail to assess the soundness and significance of the method. The lack of concrete baselines, quantitative results, and architectural clarity makes it impossible to evaluate whether the approach represents a meaningful advance over existing personalization techniques.

**Confidence:**
3 (moderate). The assessment is limited by the abstract's lack of technical depth; a full paper might provide sufficient detail to raise the score.

**Soundness:**
2 (fair). The core idea is reasonable, but without methodological details or empirical results, soundness cannot be properly evaluated.

**Presentation:**
2 (fair). The abstract is clear in motivation but vague on method and results. Key technical and experimental details are missing.

**Contribution:**
2 (fair). The dataset is a concrete contribution, but the methodological novelty and empirical validation are not evident from the abstract alone.
