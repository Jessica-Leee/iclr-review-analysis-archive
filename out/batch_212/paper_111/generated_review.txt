**Review:**

This paper addresses communication efficiency in decentralized learning by proposing a method that combines three techniques: (1) elimination of auxiliary parameter communication in adaptive optimizers, (2) multiple local model updates before transmission, and (3) extension of sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) to random-walk optimization. The authors claim faster convergence than existing approaches at the same communication cost and provide theoretical analysis alongside empirical validation on multiple datasets and network configurations.

**Strengths:**

- **Clear practical motivation**: The paper identifies a genuine bottleneck in decentralized adaptive optimization—communication of momentum and preconditioner states—and proposes a straightforward solution that is easy to implement.
- **Multi-faceted approach**: Combining three complementary techniques (eliminating auxiliary communication, local updates, and SAM extension) is sensible and well-motivated for reducing communication overhead.
- **Theoretical contribution**: The paper provides convergence analysis demonstrating faster convergence than existing methods under the same communication budget, which is a non-trivial theoretical result.
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation**: Testing across various datasets, communication networks, and network sizes suggests broad applicability.

**Weaknesses:**

- **Limited novelty of individual components**: While the combination is useful, each component is relatively incremental—eliminating auxiliary parameter communication and performing multiple local updates are standard techniques in federated/decentralized learning. The SAM extension, while novel, is a relatively straightforward adaptation.
- **Insufficient detail on theoretical analysis**: The abstract provides no specifics on convergence rates, assumptions, or how the theoretical guarantees compare quantitatively to baselines. Without seeing the full paper, it is unclear whether the theoretical improvement is substantial or marginal.
- **Vague empirical claims**: The abstract states the method "outperforms existing approaches while significantly reducing communication costs" but provides no concrete numbers, baselines, or datasets. It is unclear which specific methods are compared and by how much.
- **Missing ablation and analysis**: The abstract does not clarify the individual contribution of each of the three components. Are all three necessary? Which provides the most benefit? This limits understanding of the method's design.

**Questions:**

- What are the specific convergence rates (in terms of communication rounds and/or gradient complexity) compared to existing random-walk optimizers?
- How much communication reduction is achieved in practice? What is the trade-off between local updates and convergence speed?
- Can you provide ablation studies isolating the contribution of each component (auxiliary parameter elimination, local updates, SAM)?
- How does the method perform in heterogeneous (non-IID) data settings, which are common in decentralized learning?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a relevant problem in decentralized learning and proposes a practical solution with theoretical backing. However, the individual components are relatively standard, and the abstract lacks sufficient detail on theoretical guarantees and empirical improvements to fully assess the contribution. The work appears solid but incremental; acceptance would depend on the quality of the theoretical analysis and the magnitude of empirical improvements shown in the full paper.
