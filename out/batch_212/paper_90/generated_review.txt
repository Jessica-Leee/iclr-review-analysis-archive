**Review:**

This paper presents the first efficient algorithm for learning halfspaces in the testable learning model of Rubinfeld and Vasilyan [2022]. The key contribution is a tester-learner that certifies near-optimal accuracy whenever the training set passes an associated test, with guarantees that training sets from the target distribution (standard Gaussian or strongly log-concave) must pass the test. For Massart noise, the algorithm achieves information-theoretically optimal error opt+ε in polynomial time; for adversarial noise, it achieves O(opt)+ε. The main technical innovation is developing label-aware tests that complement the moment-matching approach of Gollakota et al. [2022], enabling a testable variant of the nonconvex SGD algorithm from Diakonikolas et al. [2020a, 2020b].

**Strengths:**

1. **Novel and challenging problem setting:** The testable learning model is significantly more demanding than standard distribution-specific or agnostic learning, as the learner must certify its guarantees and cannot fail arbitrarily when distributional assumptions are violated. Solving this for halfspaces is a meaningful theoretical contribution.

2. **Strong theoretical guarantees:** Achieving information-theoretically optimal error (opt+ε) for Massart noise and O(opt)+ε for adversarial noise in polynomial time represents solid progress. The extension to strongly log-concave distributions adds generality.

3. **Technical innovation:** The development of label-aware tests is a genuine contribution beyond prior work (which ignored labels). Combining these with moment-matching and nonconvex SGD is a principled approach that bridges distribution testing and learning.

4. **Clarity of contribution:** The paper clearly delineates what is new (label-aware tests, testable variant of existing algorithms) versus what builds on prior work, making the contribution transparent.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Limited experimental validation:** The abstract provides no mention of empirical evaluation. For a learning paper, even synthetic experiments demonstrating the algorithm's practical behavior, sample complexity, and comparison to baselines would strengthen the contribution.

2. **Restriction to specific distributions:** While the Gaussian case is natural and the extension to log-concave distributions is nice, the applicability is limited. The paper does not discuss whether the approach generalizes to other natural distributions or what fundamental barriers might exist.

3. **Gap between Massart and adversarial noise:** The error guarantee for adversarial noise is O(opt)+ε versus opt+ε for Massart noise. While this may be unavoidable, the paper does not clearly explain whether this gap is fundamental or an artifact of the algorithm design.

4. **Incomplete technical details in abstract:** The abstract does not provide sufficient detail on how the label-aware tests work or what makes them "critical" to the approach. More intuition about the key technical ideas would aid understanding.

**Questions:**

1. Can you provide intuition for why label-aware tests are necessary? What would fail if one used only moment-matching on covariates?
2. Is the O(opt) factor for adversarial noise information-theoretically necessary, or is it an algorithmic limitation?
3. Have you conducted any experiments, even on synthetic data, to validate the practical performance of your tester-learner?
4. Does the approach extend to learning other concept classes beyond halfspaces, or are there fundamental barriers?
