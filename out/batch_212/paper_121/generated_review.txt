**Review:**

This paper proposes Diffusion Visual Programmer (DVP), a neuro-symbolic framework for image translation that embeds a condition-flexible diffusion model within a GPT architecture to orchestrate a sequence of visual programs (computer vision models) for tasks such as RoI identification, style transfer, and position manipulation. The authors claim three main contributions: (1) condition-flexible translation via instance normalization to reduce sensitivity to manual guidance, (2) enhanced in-context reasoning by converting high-dimensional concepts into low-dimensional symbols (e.g., [Prompt], [RoI object]), and (3) improved controllability and explainability through explicit symbolic representations at each programming stage.

**Strengths:** The core idea of combining diffusion models with symbolic visual programming is conceptually appealing and addresses a real need for interpretability and controllability in image translation. The framing of image translation as a sequence of explicit symbolic steps is intuitive and could enable more transparent editing workflows. The paper claims to outperform concurrent methods on unspecified benchmarks.

**Weaknesses:** The abstract provides insufficient technical detail to assess the soundness of the approach. Critical information is missing: (1) How exactly is the condition-flexible diffusion model "embedded within the GPT architecture"? What is the precise architectural integration? (2) How does instance normalization specifically achieve condition-flexibility, and what is the theoretical or empirical justification? (3) The paper claims "extensive experiments" but provides no quantitative results, dataset names, baselines, or metrics in the abstract. (4) The symbolic representation scheme ([Prompt], [RoI object]) is mentioned but not formally defined or justified. (5) No ablation studies or failure case analyses are discussed. The abstract reads more as a position statement than a complete technical contribution, making it difficult to evaluate the actual novelty and validity of the method.

**Assessment:** While the high-level vision of neuro-symbolic image translation is interesting, the abstract lacks the technical depth and empirical evidence needed for a strong contribution. The absence of concrete experimental results, architectural details, and methodological justification raises concerns about whether the work is sufficiently developed and validated for a top-tier venue. The paper would benefit from detailed descriptions of the DVP architecture, formal definitions of the symbolic representations, and comprehensive quantitative comparisons with baselines.
