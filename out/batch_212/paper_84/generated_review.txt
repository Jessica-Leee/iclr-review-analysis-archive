**Review:**

This paper addresses the Non-negative Inverse Eigenvalue Problem (NIEP) by studying when a diagonal matrix $J_{K,m}$ with specified negative entries can be transformed into a doubly stochastic matrix via orthogonal conjugation. The authors employ a combined Haar and Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) approach to show that the NIEP is solvable in most cases, claim sharpness of their results, and derive a computable NMF algorithm as a byproduct. While the problem is mathematically classical and the motivation from NMF and network modeling is reasonable, the paper suffers from significant presentation and scope issues that limit its suitability for ICLR.

**Strengths:**
- Addresses a classical problem (NIEP) with a novel combined Haar/DFT construction approach that yields explicit, computable solutions.
- Claims to provide sharp results characterizing when the problem is solvable, which would be a strong theoretical contribution.
- Proposes a concrete NMF algorithm derived from the construction, bridging theory to a practical application.
- The problem has clear motivation in non-negative matrix factorization and network modeling.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Presentation and clarity:** The abstract is dense and difficult to parse. The problem setup (the specific form of $J_{K,m}$ and the doubly stochastic constraint) is introduced without sufficient intuition or justification. It is unclear why this particular diagonal matrix structure is the focus or how it relates to general NIEP instances.
- **Missing empirical validation:** No experimental results, comparisons with existing NMF algorithms, or numerical demonstrations are provided. For a venue like ICLR, which values empirical validation, the complete absence of experiments is a critical gap.
- **Scope mismatch:** The paper reads as a pure mathematics contribution (eigenvalue problems, orthogonal matrices, doubly stochastic matrices) with limited connection to machine learning. The claimed NMF algorithm and network modeling applications are mentioned but not developed or evaluated.
- **Incomplete results description:** The abstract states results are "sharp" and solvable "in most cases" but does not specify which cases are solvable and which are not, making it impossible to assess the actual scope of the contribution.

**Questions:**
- Can you provide explicit characterization of the $(K, m)$ pairs for which the NIEP is solvable versus unsolvable?
- How does the proposed NMF algorithm compare empirically to standard methods (e.g., multiplicative updates, gradient descent) on benchmark datasets?
- What is the computational complexity of the Haar/DFT construction approach, and how does it scale?
- How do the network modeling applications concretely benefit from these results?

**Overall score:**
3: Reject, not good enough. While the paper tackles a classical mathematical problem with a novel approach, it lacks the empirical validation, clarity, and machine learning focus expected at ICLR. The contribution, though potentially solid mathematically, is not sufficiently developed or demonstrated for this venue. The paper would be better suited to a mathematics or numerical analysis conference after substantial revision to clarify results and add experimental validation.
