**Review:**

This paper proposes GMMD, a fairness-aware message passing framework for Graph Neural Networks that addresses bias in node predictions with respect to sensitive attributes. The key contribution is deriving the message passing mechanism from an optimization problem balancing graph smoothness and representation fairness, which yields an intuitive update rule: nodes aggregate representations from different sensitive groups while subtracting those from the same group. The authors provide theoretical analysis justifying fairness guarantees and propose a simpler variant GMMD-S. Experiments on graph benchmarks demonstrate improved fairness across various GNN backbones with maintained accuracy.

**Strengths:**
- **Well-motivated approach:** The paper identifies a genuine gap—that existing fairness techniques are adapted to graphs without leveraging the distinctive message passing mechanism. The proposed framework directly incorporates fairness into the aggregation step, which is intuitive and architecturally natural.
- **Theoretical grounding:** Deriving GMMD from an explicit optimization objective and providing fairness guarantees (leading to GMMD-S) strengthens the contribution beyond purely empirical work.
- **Generality:** The framework is shown to work across multiple GNN backbones, suggesting broad applicability.
- **Clear presentation:** The core idea—encouraging cross-group aggregation while penalizing within-group aggregation—is easy to understand and well-articulated.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited novelty in fairness concepts:** While the message passing formulation is novel, the underlying fairness objective (balancing graph smoothness with fairness) appears to combine standard techniques. The paper would benefit from clearer positioning relative to prior fairness-aware graph learning work.
- **Incomplete experimental details:** The abstract does not specify which graph benchmarks are used, what fairness metrics are evaluated (e.g., demographic parity, equalized odds), or how significantly accuracy is maintained. Without these details, it is difficult to assess the practical trade-off.
- **Theoretical analysis scope unclear:** The abstract mentions "theoretical analysis to justify fairness" but does not specify what fairness property is guaranteed (e.g., under what conditions, for which definition of fairness). This limits confidence in the theoretical contribution.
- **Missing ablation and sensitivity analysis:** No discussion of hyperparameter sensitivity, ablation studies comparing GMMD vs. GMMD-S, or analysis of failure cases is provided in the abstract.

**Questions:**
- What specific fairness metrics (e.g., demographic parity, equalized odds, individual fairness) does GMMD guarantee, and under what assumptions?
- How does GMMD compare quantitatively to other fairness-aware GNN methods on standard benchmarks?
- What is the computational overhead of GMMD relative to standard message passing?
- How sensitive is the method to the choice of sensitive attributes and the balance of groups in the graph?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid contribution by directly integrating fairness into the GNN message passing mechanism with theoretical justification. The approach is intuitive and shows promise for improving fairness while maintaining accuracy. However, the limited novelty in fairness concepts, incomplete experimental reporting in the abstract, and unclear scope of theoretical guarantees prevent a stronger assessment. With full experimental details and clearer positioning relative to prior work, this could be a strong accept.
