**Review:**

This paper addresses source-free cross-modal knowledge transfer, where a pre-trained source model (e.g., RGB) must adapt to a target modality (depth or infrared) without access to source training data. The key insight is to leverage paired task-irrelevant (TI) data—data available in both modalities but unrelated to the task—to better estimate the source distribution and bridge the modality gap. The authors propose two main components: (1) a Task-irrelevant data-Guided Modality Bridging (TGMB) module that translates target modality data into source-like images using paired TI data, and (2) a Task-irrelevant data-Guided Knowledge Transfer (TGKT) module that transfers knowledge via self-supervised pseudo-labeling. The approach is evaluated on RGB-to-depth and RGB-to-infrared benchmarks.

**Strengths:**
- The problem formulation is well-motivated: source-free cross-modal transfer is practically important, and the observation that paired TI data can estimate source distribution is intuitive and novel.
- The two-module design is conceptually clear: TGMB addresses both inter-modality gaps (between paired TI data) and intra-modality gaps (between TI and task-relevant target data), while TGKT leverages self-supervised learning to handle unlabeled target data.
- Evaluation on three datasets (RGB-to-depth and RGB-to-infrared) with claimed state-of-the-art results suggests practical relevance and empirical validation.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks critical implementation details. How exactly does TGMB "translate" target modality data into source-like images? Is this a generative model (e.g., GAN, diffusion)? What is the architecture and training procedure? Without these details, reproducibility and technical soundness are difficult to assess.
- The self-supervised pseudo-labeling approach in TGKT is mentioned but not explained. What specific self-supervised method is used? How are pseudo-labels generated and filtered to avoid error accumulation, especially given that source model predictions on target data are acknowledged as "less reliable"?
- The abstract does not provide ablation studies or comparisons to prior work. How much does each component (TGMB vs. TGKT) contribute? How does this compare quantitatively to the "recent attempt" that uses direct feature matching?
- Missing analysis of failure cases and limitations. When would paired TI data be insufficient or misleading? Are there modality pairs where this approach struggles?

**Questions:**
- What is the architecture of the TGMB translation module, and how is it trained without task-relevant source data?
- How does the self-supervised pseudo-labeling in TGKT prevent error accumulation in the target model?
- Can you provide quantitative ablations isolating the contribution of TGMB and TGKT?
- How sensitive is the method to the quality and quantity of paired TI data?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a relevant problem with a reasonable intuition (leveraging paired TI data for distribution estimation), and claims strong empirical results. However, the abstract is insufficiently detailed to fully evaluate the technical contribution and soundness of the proposed TGMB and TGKT modules. The lack of ablation studies and comparison details raises concerns about the clarity of individual contributions. If the full paper provides thorough implementation details, ablations, and comparisons, this could be a solid contribution; as presented in the abstract alone, it is borderline.
