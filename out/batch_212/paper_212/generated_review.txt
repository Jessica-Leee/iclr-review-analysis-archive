**Review:**

PolyGCL addresses an important limitation of existing Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) methods: their bias toward homophilic graphs due to low-pass GNN encoders and homophily-based objectives. The paper proposes incorporating learnable spectral polynomial filters into GCL to generate both low-pass and high-pass spectral views, enabling contrastive learning that adapts to graphs with varying homophily levels. The key innovation is using polynomials with learnable filter functions to create diverse spectral views and combining them through a linear combination objective that incorporates high-pass information. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing PolyGCL outperforms prior GCL paradigms across homophilic and heterophilic settings, and validate the approach on synthetic and real-world datasets.

**Strengths:**
- Well-motivated problem: the paper clearly identifies a genuine limitation of existing GCL methods (their smoothing bias) and connects it to spectral properties, providing solid intuition for why polynomial filters are a natural solution.
- Theoretically grounded: the paper claims theoretical proofs that PolyGCL outperforms previous GCL paradigms under varying homophily conditions, which strengthens the contribution beyond empirical validation.
- Comprehensive scope: the approach is evaluated on both homophilic and heterophilic graphs, demonstrating broad applicability rather than solving a narrow problem.
- Learnable design: using learnable polynomial filters rather than fixed spectral operations allows the method to adapt to different graph structures, which is more flexible than hand-crafted spectral approaches.

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited methodological detail in abstract: the abstract does not explain how the polynomial filters are parameterized, how the learnable parameters are optimized, or how the linear combination of low-pass and high-pass views is weighted. These details are crucial for reproducibility and understanding the method's complexity.
- Vague theoretical contribution: the abstract states "theoretically prove that PolyGCL outperforms previous GCL paradigms" but provides no sketch of the proof strategy, assumptions, or the form of the theoretical result. Without seeing the full paper, it is unclear whether this is a strong theoretical guarantee or a weaker statement.
- Incomplete experimental description: the abstract mentions "extensive experiments" but does not specify which datasets, baselines, or metrics are used. It is unclear whether comparisons include recent heterophilic GCL methods or only standard baselines.
- Missing ablation and analysis: the abstract does not discuss ablation studies (e.g., impact of polynomial degree, weighting of spectral views) or provide intuition for when and why the method works better than alternatives.

**Questions:**
- How are the polynomial filter parameters learned during training? Are they optimized jointly with the GNN encoder?
- What is the computational overhead of PolyGCL compared to standard GCL methods, particularly for large graphs?
- How sensitive is the method to the degree of the polynomial approximation?
- Can you provide a concrete example of how the low-pass and high-pass views differ for a heterophilic graph?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a relevant problem in graph representation learning and proposes a principled solution grounded in spectral analysis. The motivation is clear and the idea of using learnable polynomial filters for heterophilic graphs is sound. However, the abstract lacks sufficient methodological and experimental detail to fully assess the contribution's significance. The theoretical claims are interesting but not substantiated in the abstract. With a complete paper providing clear algorithmic details, thorough experiments, and rigorous theoretical analysis, this could be a solid contribution; based on the abstract alone, it merits marginal acceptance pending verification of the claims.
