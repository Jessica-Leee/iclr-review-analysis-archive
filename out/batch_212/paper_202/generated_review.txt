**Review:**

SPI-GAN proposes a GAN-based denoising method that aims to reduce the computational cost of score-based generative models (SGMs) while maintaining comparable sampling quality and diversity. The core contribution is the introduction of straight-path interpolation, a simpler denoising trajectory that replaces the complex forward/reverse processes of SGMs. The method combines a GAN architecture with a continuous mapping neural network to imitate this denoising path, with the stated goal of achieving faster sampling than SGMs on standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ-256, LSUN-Church-256).

**Strengths:**
- The motivation is clear and timely: reducing the sampling complexity of diffusion models while maintaining quality is an important practical problem.
- The paper targets a well-defined comparison (SGMs vs. GANs) on established benchmarks, suggesting empirical validation across multiple datasets.
- The proposed straight-path interpolation concept offers a potentially simpler alternative to the standard diffusion process.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks crucial technical detail about what "straight-path interpolation" actually means mathematically. Without a formal definition, it is impossible to assess the novelty or soundness of the core contribution.
- No quantitative results, comparisons, or ablation studies are provided in the abstract. Claims that SPI-GAN is "one of the best-balanced models" are unsupported by any metrics (FID, IS, sampling time, etc.).
- The relationship between the GAN component and the denoising path is unclear. How does the GAN loss interact with the straight-path objective? Why is a GAN necessary rather than, say, a direct regression approach?
- The abstract does not discuss related work on GAN-based diffusion hybrids or other recent attempts to accelerate diffusion sampling (e.g., consistency models, progressive distillation), making it difficult to position the novelty.

**Questions:**
- Can you provide a formal mathematical definition of straight-path interpolation and explain how it differs from standard linear or geodesic interpolations in diffusion models?
- What are the quantitative FID, IS, and wall-clock sampling time comparisons against SGMs and recent fast-sampling baselines?
- How does the GAN discriminator contribute to learning the denoising path, and what is the training stability like compared to standard diffusion or GAN training?
- Have you performed ablations to isolate the contribution of straight-path interpolation versus the GAN architecture?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem, but the abstract provides insufficient technical detail and no empirical evidence to support its claims. The core novelty (straight-path interpolation) is not defined clearly enough to evaluate, and the lack of quantitative results makes it impossible to assess whether the method actually achieves its stated goals. A full paper with formal definitions, comprehensive experiments, and detailed comparisons is needed.
