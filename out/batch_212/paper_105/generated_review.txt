**Review:**

Matcher proposes a novel perception paradigm that leverages off-the-shelf vision foundation models for one-shot segmentation across diverse tasks without task-specific training. The core idea is to use all-purpose feature matching with in-context examples to enable zero-shot or few-shot segmentation. The paper claims strong empirical results: 52.7% mIoU on COCO-20^i (1.6% above prior art) and 33.0% mIoU on a newly proposed LVIS-92^i benchmark (14.4% above a generalist baseline). The authors design three effective components within the Matcher framework to enhance foundation model performance on perception tasks.

**Strengths:**
- The motivation is compelling: adapting vision foundation models to diverse segmentation tasks without fine-tuning addresses a practical gap between vision and language models.
- Strong empirical results on established benchmarks (COCO-20^i) and a newly introduced benchmark (LVIS-92^i) demonstrate competitive performance.
- The approach is general and appears to work across multiple segmentation tasks, suggesting good generalization.
- Visualization results on open-world images suggest practical applicability.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides insufficient technical detail about the "three effective components" and how feature matching is implemented. Without understanding the core methodology, it is difficult to assess novelty and soundness.
- No comparison of computational cost or inference time against specialist and generalist baselines is mentioned, which is important for practical deployment.
- The paper introduces a new benchmark (LVIS-92^i) but provides no justification for its design or evidence that it is representative of real-world one-shot segmentation challenges.
- Limited discussion of failure cases, limitations, or when Matcher underperforms relative to task-specific models.
- The claim of "segment anything" is overstated given the focus on segmentation tasks; clarity on scope would strengthen the contribution.

**Questions:**
- What are the three key components of Matcher, and how do they differ from existing feature matching or in-context learning approaches?
- How does Matcher's inference time and memory footprint compare to specialist and generalist baselines?
- What is the design rationale for LVIS-92^i, and how was it constructed from LVIS?
- How sensitive is performance to the choice of in-context example?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and reports strong empirical results on multiple benchmarks. However, the abstract lacks sufficient methodological detail to fully evaluate novelty and soundness. The introduction of a new benchmark without clear justification and the absence of computational cost analysis are concerns. With clearer exposition of the three core components and more thorough ablation studies, this could be a solid contribution.
