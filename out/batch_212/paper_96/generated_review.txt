**Review:**

This paper reframes embedding-based entity alignment (EEA) from knowledge graphs through the lens of generative models. The authors argue that recent GAN-based EEA methods, while effective, have incomplete objectives that limit both entity alignment and entity synthesis. They propose a generative EEA (GEEA) framework using a mutual variational autoencoder (M-VAE) to address these limitations, enabling entity conversion between KGs and generation of new entities. The work combines theoretical analysis with empirical validation on both entity alignment and entity synthesis tasks.

**Strengths:**
- Novel perspective: Reframing EEA as a generative modeling problem is conceptually interesting and provides fresh theoretical insight into why existing GAN-based methods work.
- Dual contribution: The paper addresses both entity alignment and entity synthesis in a unified framework, extending the scope beyond typical alignment-only approaches.
- Theoretical grounding: The authors provide theoretical analysis to justify their approach and explain limitations of prior GAN-based methods.
- Reproducibility: Code and datasets are made available, supporting verification and future work.

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited methodological novelty: While the perspective is fresh, M-VAE appears to be a relatively straightforward application of variational autoencoders to the EEA setting. The technical innovation beyond standard VAE formulations is unclear.
- Incomplete experimental details: The abstract lacks specifics on datasets, baselines, and quantitative results. Without seeing empirical comparisons against state-of-the-art methods, it is difficult to assess whether GEEA achieves meaningful improvements.
- Entity synthesis evaluation unclear: The paper claims to address entity synthesis, but the practical utility and evaluation methodology for this task are not explained in the abstract. How is synthetic entity quality measured?
- Presentation: The abstract is dense and somewhat difficult to parse. The connection between the theoretical analysis of GAN limitations and the M-VAE solution could be articulated more clearly.

**Questions:**
- How does GEEA compare quantitatively to recent GAN-based and other state-of-the-art EEA methods on standard benchmarks?
- What is the practical motivation for entity synthesis in the KG alignment context? How is this task evaluated?
- Can you provide more detail on how M-VAE differs from standard VAE formulations for this problem?
- How does the mutual aspect of M-VAE specifically address the "incomplete objective" problem you identify in GAN-based methods?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper presents an interesting theoretical perspective on EEA and proposes a principled generative approach with dual objectives. However, the methodological contribution is somewhat incremental, and the abstract lacks sufficient empirical evidence to strongly support the claims. The work would benefit from clearer presentation and more detailed experimental validation. The availability of code and the theoretical grounding are positive factors, but the limited novelty in the M-VAE architecture itself and unclear practical benefits of entity synthesis prevent a stronger recommendation.
