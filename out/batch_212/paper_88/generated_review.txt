**Review:**

This paper addresses the "Assessment Misalignment" problem in fine-tuned LLMs, where models assign higher scores to subpar chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning processes. The authors propose Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT), a paradigm with a novel Constrained Alignment Loss that combines two objectives: (1) alignment, ensuring high-quality COTs receive higher scores than subpar ones, and (2) constraint, preventing subpar scores from degrading model performance. The method is evaluated on four reasoning benchmarks with both binary and ranking feedback, and the authors also analyze recent ranking-based alignment methods (DPO, RRHF, PRO) to show that the constraint component is underexplored but crucial.

**Strengths:**
- The identification of assessment misalignment as a concrete problem in COT fine-tuning is well-motivated and practically relevant for reasoning tasks.
- The proposed Constrained Alignment Loss is simple yet intuitive, with clear dual objectives that address both alignment and stability.
- Comprehensive experimental validation across multiple benchmarks and feedback types (binary and ranking), plus multi-task and out-of-distribution evaluation, demonstrates broad applicability.
- The secondary contribution—showing that constraint is important for existing ranking-based alignment methods—provides useful insights for the community.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks sufficient technical depth and novelty. The Constrained Alignment Loss appears to be a straightforward combination of margin-based ranking and score regularization; the core innovation is incremental relative to existing alignment methods.
- Limited ablation studies and analysis: the paper does not clearly isolate the contribution of the alignment vs. constraint components, nor does it provide intuition for why constraint prevents degradation beyond empirical observation.
- The writing and presentation could be clearer. Key details about the loss formulation, hyperparameter selection (e.g., constraint bounds), and experimental setup are not fully specified in the abstract, making reproducibility uncertain.
- Missing comparisons with other recent reasoning-focused fine-tuning methods and limited discussion of computational cost or scalability implications.

**Questions:**
- How sensitive is AFT to the choice of constraint bounds? Is there a principled way to set these hyperparameters, or is tuning required per task?
- Can you provide more detailed ablations showing the individual contribution of alignment and constraint components?
- How does AFT compare to simpler baselines, such as standard supervised fine-tuning with explicit score normalization?
- What is the computational overhead of AFT relative to standard fine-tuning?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper makes a solid practical contribution by identifying and addressing assessment misalignment in COT fine-tuning, with reasonable experimental validation. However, the technical novelty is limited—the Constrained Alignment Loss is a relatively straightforward extension of existing ideas—and the presentation lacks depth in explaining why the constraint component is crucial. The secondary insight about ranking-based methods is useful but not sufficient to elevate the contribution significantly. The work is competent and likely useful for practitioners, but falls short of being a strong accept.
