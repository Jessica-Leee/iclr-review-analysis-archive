**Review:**

This paper proposes TCD (Text image Change Detection), a model for multilingual document comparison that avoids OCR by performing unit-level text image-to-image comparison. The method generates bidirectional change segmentation maps and leverages correlation between multi-scale attention features to mitigate the need for explicit text image position and scale alignment. The authors evaluate on a combination of newly created test data from printed and scanned documents and public datasets (DDI-100, LRDE DBD), comparing against semantic segmentation, change detection, and OCR baselines.

**Strengths:**
- Addresses a practical problem: multilingual document comparison without language-specific OCR models is a genuine challenge in document processing.
- The core idea of image-to-image comparison rather than text recognition is intuitive and avoids multilingual OCR bottlenecks.
- Evaluation includes both newly created multilingual data and established public benchmarks (DDI-100, LRDE DBD).
- Bidirectional change segmentation (source→target and target→source) is a reasonable design choice for symmetry.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides insufficient technical detail about the model architecture, attention mechanism, and how multi-scale feature correlation is computed. The phrase "correlation between multi-scale attention features" is vague and lacks specificity.
- No clear comparison of computational cost or inference speed versus OCR-based methods, which is important for practical deployment.
- The claim that the model "meets similar performance that of OCR methods" is ambiguous—does this mean comparable accuracy, or does it underperform? The phrasing suggests the method may not actually outperform OCR baselines.
- The abstract does not specify which semantic segmentation or change detection baselines are used, making it difficult to assess the significance of the claimed improvements. The phrase "outperforms other semantic segmentation models relatively by a good margin" lacks quantitative support.
- Limited discussion of failure cases, limitations, or when image-to-image comparison might be inferior to OCR (e.g., for text extraction tasks).

**Questions:**
- What is the exact architecture of the multi-scale attention mechanism, and how is feature correlation computed?
- Can you provide quantitative comparisons (e.g., mIoU, F1 scores) against specific baselines on each dataset?
- How does inference time and memory usage compare to OCR-based and semantic segmentation baselines?
- How does the method handle documents with very different layouts or scales between source and target?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. While the problem is well-motivated and the core idea has merit, the abstract lacks sufficient technical clarity and quantitative validation. The vague claims about performance ("meets similar performance," "good margin") and missing architectural details make it impossible to assess the contribution's significance. A full paper with detailed methodology, ablation studies, and clear quantitative comparisons would be needed to evaluate this work properly.
