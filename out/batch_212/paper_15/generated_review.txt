**Review:**

This paper proposes Epistemic-MCTS (E-MCTS), an extension of Monte-Carlo Tree Search that incorporates estimation and propagation of epistemic uncertainty to enable deep exploration in model-based reinforcement learning. The core contribution is a novel planning algorithm that explicitly leverages propagated epistemic uncertainty to guide exploration, integrated into both MuZero (learned dynamics) and AlphaZero (provided dynamics) variants. The authors evaluate E-MCTS against non-planning deep-exploration baselines on a deep exploration benchmark and report significant performance improvements.

**Strengths:**
- The motivation is well-grounded: deep exploration remains a genuine challenge in MCTS-based MBRL methods like MuZero, and addressing it through principled uncertainty propagation is a natural and valuable direction.
- The approach is technically sound in concept—extending MCTS with epistemic uncertainty estimates and using them to guide tree search is a coherent idea that bridges planning and exploration.
- The paper demonstrates applicability to both learned and provided dynamics models, suggesting generality.
- Empirical results show substantial improvements over non-planning baselines on the deep exploration benchmark.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract and summary lack sufficient technical detail about *how* epistemic uncertainty is estimated, propagated, and used in the planning algorithm. The mechanism for "explicitly planning to explore" is not clearly described, making it difficult to assess novelty and reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to a single "deep exploration benchmark." Without results on standard RL benchmarks (e.g., Atari, MuJoCo, or other domains), it is unclear whether E-MCTS generalizes beyond the specific exploration regime tested or whether improvements are merely domain-specific.
- No comparison to planning-based exploration methods (e.g., other uncertainty-driven planning approaches or recent variants of MuZero with exploration mechanisms), only non-planning baselines. This limits insight into whether the planning component itself is the key advantage.
- The paper does not discuss computational overhead: propagating and maintaining uncertainty estimates throughout the tree likely increases per-step cost, but this is not analyzed or reported.

**Questions:**
- How is epistemic uncertainty estimated in practice (e.g., ensemble methods, Bayesian neural networks, other)? How sensitive is performance to this choice?
- Can you provide results on standard RL benchmarks to demonstrate generalization beyond the deep exploration setting?
- What is the computational cost of E-MCTS relative to standard MCTS, and how does it scale with tree depth and breadth?
- How does E-MCTS compare to other planning-based or uncertainty-driven exploration methods in the literature?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a reasonable solution with positive empirical results. However, the presentation lacks clarity on key technical details, and the evaluation is narrow (single benchmark, non-planning baselines only). With revisions to clarify the method and broader experimental validation, this could be a solid contribution.
