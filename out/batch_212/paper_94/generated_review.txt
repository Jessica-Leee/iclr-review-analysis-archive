**Review:**

This paper proposes Random Orthogonal Projection Image Modeling (ROPIM), a self-supervised pre-training method that replaces binary masking in standard Masked Image Modeling (MIM) with random orthogonal projection. The key idea is to project image tokens into a random subspace, thereby reducing spatial information under a controlled noise variance bound, and then use the orthogonal complement of that subspace to recover masked information during decoding. The authors claim this approach yields superior performance compared to crop-based masking on several benchmarks.

**Strengths:**
1. The core idea of using random orthogonal projections as a principled alternative to binary masking is conceptually interesting and well-motivated—the guarantee on noise variance bounds and the natural availability of the orthogonal complement provide theoretical grounding.
2. The method is presented as a general framework that can be applied to existing MIM architectures, suggesting practical applicability.
3. The paper claims state-of-the-art results on popular benchmarks, indicating potential empirical impact.

**Weaknesses:**
1. The abstract provides insufficient detail on the actual experimental setup, datasets, baselines, and quantitative results. Without seeing the full paper, it is impossible to verify the claimed "state-of-the-art" performance or assess whether improvements are statistically significant and practically meaningful.
2. The connection between the theoretical motivation (noise variance bounds, orthogonal complements) and the actual implementation is unclear from the abstract alone. How exactly does the orthogonal complement improve recovery compared to standard decoder designs? Is the improvement purely from the projection structure, or does it depend on architectural choices?
3. The abstract does not discuss computational cost or efficiency. Random orthogonal projection and its complement may introduce overhead; comparison of training time and memory usage relative to standard MIM is essential.
4. The claim that ROPIM can be viewed as "masking entire spatial image area under locally varying masking degrees" needs clarification. How does this relate to existing variable masking strategies, and what is the advantage over simpler alternatives?

**Questions:**
- What are the specific quantitative improvements over MAE and other recent MIM methods on ImageNet and downstream tasks?
- How sensitive is ROPIM to the choice of random subspace dimension and projection method?
- What is the computational overhead compared to standard MIM?
- Have you compared against other principled masking strategies (e.g., learned or adaptive masking)?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper presents a novel and theoretically motivated approach to self-supervised pre-training with a clear conceptual contribution. However, the abstract alone does not provide sufficient empirical evidence or implementation details to fully assess the work. The claimed state-of-the-art results and the principled use of orthogonal projections are promising, but stronger experimental validation, clearer exposition of the method's advantages, and discussion of computational trade-offs would strengthen the contribution. The work appears solid but incremental; acceptance hinges on the quality of the full experimental section.
