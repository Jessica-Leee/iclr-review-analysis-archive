**Review:**

This paper proposes a hierarchical generative modeling approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that aims to reduce dependence on manually designed search spaces. The method employs a two-level hierarchy: (1) a Conditional Continuous Normalizing Flow (CCNF) for micro-cell design, and (2) a transformer-based sequence generator for macro-architecture generation. To make the approach computationally tractable, the authors perform task-agnostic pretraining using zero-cost (ZC) similarity in a metric space of graphs. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, claiming state-of-the-art performance among low-cost NAS approaches.

**Strengths:**
- The core motivation is sound: reducing prior knowledge dependency in NAS by leveraging generative models is a worthwhile direction that could improve applicability to less-studied domains.
- The hierarchical decomposition into micro-cell and macro-architecture levels is intuitive and aligns with established NAS conventions.
- Task-agnostic pretraining using zero-cost similarity is a practical strategy to reduce computational cost, addressing a key bottleneck in NAS.
- Evaluation on standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet) provides reasonable empirical grounding.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks sufficient technical detail to assess the novelty and soundness of the approach. How exactly does the CCNF operate on micro-cells? What is the precise formulation of the zero-cost similarity metric, and how is it used during pretraining?
- No comparison with recent strong NAS baselines (e.g., DARTS, ENAS, or other low-cost methods) is mentioned in the abstract. The claim of "state-of-the-art performance compared to other low-cost NAS approaches" is unsupported without concrete numbers or references.
- The computational cost of the method itself is not discussed. While zero-cost pretraining is mentioned, the total wall-clock time and memory requirements relative to baselines are unclear.
- The abstract does not explain how the method handles the tension between generality (avoiding hand-designed search spaces) and task-specificity (the transformer generator is "for a given task and architectural constraints"). This seems to reintroduce task-specific design choices.

**Questions:**
- How does the CCNF-based micro-cell generation ensure that generated cells are valid and compatible with standard NAS frameworks?
- What is the sensitivity of the method to the choice of zero-cost proxy? Have alternative proxies been explored?
- How does performance scale with search space size? Does the method truly navigate an "extremely large" space, or is it still constrained in practice?
- Can you provide concrete comparisons (accuracy, latency, search cost) against recent low-cost NAS methods on the same benchmarks?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes an interesting hierarchical generative approach, but the abstract provides insufficient detail to evaluate the technical contribution rigorously. The lack of concrete experimental comparisons and unclear handling of the generality-vs.-specificity trade-off raise concerns about the practical impact. A full paper with detailed methodology, comprehensive baselines, and thorough ablations would be needed to assess this work fairly.
