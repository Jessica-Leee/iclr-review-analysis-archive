**Review:**

This paper proposes Fourier NODEs (FNODEs), a simulation-free framework for training neural ordinary differential equations that avoids standard NODE challenges by directly matching the target vector field using Fourier analysis. The core idea is to estimate temporal and spatial gradients from noisy observational data via Fourier analysis, incorporate estimated spatial gradients as additional neural network inputs, and use the estimated temporal gradient as the optimization objective. The authors claim this creates a positive feedback loop where the trained network generates additional data points through an ODE solver (outside the computational graph), enabling more accurate gradient estimation in subsequent iterations. The method is evaluated on representative complex systems and reportedly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in training time, prediction accuracy, and robustness.

**Strengths:**
- The core motivation is sound: avoiding expensive backpropagation through ODE solvers and the local optima problem in standard NODE training are genuine challenges.
- Using Fourier analysis to estimate gradients from noisy data is a principled approach with clear mathematical grounding.
- The claimed positive feedback loop between gradient estimation and data generation is conceptually interesting and could offer practical benefits.
- The paper addresses an important problem in scientific machine learning.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks critical implementation details. How exactly does the "positive feedback loop" work across iterations? How many iterations are needed? What is the computational complexity compared to standard NODE training?
- No experimental details are provided: which datasets or complex systems are used? What are the baseline methods? What are the quantitative improvements (e.g., speedup factors, error metrics)?
- The claim that the ODE solver operates "without participating in the computational graph" needs clarification—how does this avoid backpropagation entirely while still enabling end-to-end learning?
- The paper does not discuss potential failure modes or limitations of Fourier-based gradient estimation, especially for systems with discontinuities, sharp transitions, or non-periodic dynamics.
- Without concrete experimental results, reproducibility and validation of the claimed advantages (training time, robustness) cannot be assessed.

**Questions:**
1. Can you provide concrete experimental results (tables/figures) comparing FNODEs to standard NODEs and other baselines on specific datasets?
2. How sensitive is the method to the choice of Fourier basis and the noise level in observational data?
3. Does the method require periodic or smooth dynamics, or can it handle stiff systems and discontinuities?
4. What is the actual wall-clock speedup and memory usage compared to adjoint-based NODE training?

**Overall score:**
3: Reject, not good enough. While the core idea of using Fourier analysis to avoid expensive backpropagation through ODE solvers is interesting, the abstract provides insufficient detail to evaluate the method's soundness, novelty, or empirical validity. The absence of experimental results, baseline comparisons, and implementation details makes it impossible to assess whether the claimed improvements are real. The paper reads more like an early-stage idea than a complete contribution ready for publication at a top venue.
