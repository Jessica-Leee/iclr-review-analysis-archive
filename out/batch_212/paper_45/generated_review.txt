**Review:**

This paper proposes ODNet, a neural message passing scheme for graph neural networks that incorporates bounded confidence from opinion dynamics in sociology. The core idea is to refine influence weights of neighboring nodes during message propagation by adjusting similarity cutoffs and opinion exchange rules, inspired by how social actors reach consensus through selective interaction. The authors claim ODNet improves prediction performance across graph types, mitigates oversmoothing, and demonstrates utility on real-world metabolic gene co-occurrence networks by identifying community structure and node roles.

**Strengths:**
- The conceptual bridge between opinion dynamics and neural message passing is intuitive and potentially valuable for understanding information flow in social networks.
- The paper addresses a known problem (oversmoothing) in graph neural networks with a theoretically motivated approach rooted in sociological principles.
- Application to metabolic gene networks suggests practical relevance beyond standard benchmarks.
- The use of bounded confidence to modulate edge weights is a concrete, implementable modification to standard message passing.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract lacks sufficient methodological detail to assess the technical contribution. How exactly are similarity cutoffs and influence weights adjusted? What is the precise form of the opinion exchange rule? Without these specifics, reproducibility and clarity are compromised.
- No experimental results, datasets, or quantitative comparisons are provided in the abstract. Claims about "enhancing prediction performance" and "surpassing conventional baselines" are unsupported by numbers, making it impossible to evaluate empirical validity.
- The connection between bounded confidence (a mechanism for consensus in opinion dynamics) and oversmoothing mitigation in GNNs is asserted but not explained. Why should bounded confidence reduce oversmoothing?
- The paper does not clarify how the method scales or whether it introduces additional hyperparameters (e.g., confidence bounds) that require tuning, which could complicate practical deployment.

**Questions:**
- Can you provide the mathematical formulation of the bounded confidence mechanism and how it modifies the message passing equation?
- What are the quantitative improvements on standard benchmarks (e.g., citation networks, social networks) compared to GCN, GraphSAGE, and other baselines?
- How sensitive is ODNet to the choice of similarity cutoff threshold, and how is this parameter selected in practice?
- How does computational complexity compare to standard message passing?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. While the conceptual motivation is interesting, the abstract provides insufficient technical and empirical detail to support the claims. The lack of concrete results, precise methodology, and clear theoretical justification for why bounded confidence addresses oversmoothing makes it difficult to assess the paper's contribution. A full paper with detailed experiments and ablations would be needed to evaluate this work properly.
