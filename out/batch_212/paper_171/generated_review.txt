**Review:**

This paper introduces amicable perturbations, a framework for finding efficient real-world changes that would lead to more favorable classifier predictions. The authors position amicable perturbations as distinct from both counterfactuals (which optimize effort-reward trade-offs more effectively) and adversarial examples (which fool classifiers rather than change true class probabilities). The core contribution is a principled methodology for generating such perturbations and a verification method to confirm that they genuinely alter true class probabilities, with empirical validation across multiple datasets.

The paper addresses a practically motivated problem with clear real-world relevance, particularly in high-stakes domains like hiring and lending. The conceptual distinction between amicable perturbations and existing approaches (counterfactuals and adversarial examples) is intuitive and well-articulated. However, the abstract lacks crucial technical details about the proposed methodology—no specific algorithm, optimization formulation, or verification procedure is described. The claim that amicable perturbations "balance the effort-reward trade-off" better than counterfactuals is stated but not formally justified or empirically demonstrated in the abstract. Additionally, the paper provides no quantitative results, baseline comparisons, or dataset details, making it impossible to assess the actual effectiveness of the approach or the rigor of the experimental validation.

The work appears to tackle an important problem with a reasonable conceptual framework, but the abstract is too vague to evaluate the technical soundness or empirical contribution. The paper would benefit from concrete algorithmic details, formal problem formulation, and specific experimental results comparing amicable perturbations to counterfactuals and adversarial methods on named benchmarks.
