**Review:**

This paper proposes iHyperTime, a hypernetwork architecture for time series generation that combines two novel components: TSNet, an implicit neural representation (INR) network designed for interpretable trend-seasonality decomposition, and a hypernetwork that leverages TSNet for generalization and synthesis. The authors position their work as addressing a gap in applying INRs to time series, and claim advantages in handling long and irregularly sampled sequences while maintaining competitive performance on regularly sampled data.

**Strengths:**
- **Novel application of INRs to time series**: The paper addresses a genuine gap by adapting implicit neural representations to time series generation, with a principled design (TSNet) that incorporates domain knowledge through explicit trend-seasonality decomposition.
- **Comprehensive evaluation across multiple scenarios**: The evaluation covers fidelity and usefulness metrics, and importantly includes challenging cases (long and irregularly sampled sequences) where the method shows clear improvements.
- **Computational efficiency**: The reported training speed advantages, especially for longer sequences, are practically valuable and well-motivated by the INR framework.
- **Unsupervised decomposition validation**: Empirical comparison of trend-seasonality decomposition against the established STL method provides interpretability validation.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Limited methodological detail in abstract**: The abstract does not explain how TSNet achieves interpretable decomposition or how the hypernetwork architecture specifically enables generalization. The technical novelty beyond applying existing INR techniques to time series remains unclear.
- **Incomplete baseline and dataset specification**: The abstract mentions "state-of-the-art methods" and "challenging scenarios" without naming specific baselines or datasets, making it difficult to assess the significance of claimed improvements.
- **Vague claims about interpretability**: While the paper emphasizes "interpretable" representation, the abstract provides no concrete evidence or mechanism for how interpretability is achieved or measured beyond STL comparison.
- **Missing ablation and generalization analysis**: No discussion of ablation studies (e.g., contribution of trend-seasonality decomposition vs. hypernetwork) or analysis of how well the method generalizes to different time series characteristics.

**Questions:**
- How does TSNet's trend-seasonality decomposition differ from or improve upon classical methods like STL? What is the architectural mechanism that enables interpretability?
- What specific state-of-the-art baselines were compared, and on which datasets? How substantial are the improvements on regularly sampled data?
- Does the hypernetwork learn a meaningful latent space for time series? Can you provide evidence of generalization across different domains or time series types?
- How sensitive is the method to hyperparameter choices, and how does it perform on very short sequences?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a reasonable solution combining INRs with domain-specific design for time series. The claimed advantages on long and irregular sequences are appealing, and the computational efficiency is a practical benefit. However, the abstract lacks sufficient technical detail and specificity about baselines and datasets to fully assess novelty and impact. The work appears sound in principle but would benefit from clearer exposition of the technical contributions and more transparent experimental setup. With full paper details, this could be a solid contribution, but the abstract alone leaves important questions unanswered.
