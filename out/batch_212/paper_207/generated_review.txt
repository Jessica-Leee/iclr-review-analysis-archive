**Review:**

This paper proposes AdaMergeX, a method for cross-lingual transfer in Large Language Models that decomposes task performance into "task ability" and "language ability." The core idea is to merge adapters in a structured, adaptive manner: task ability is captured by adapters fine-tuned on the target task in the source language, while language ability is obtained from a reference task and merged via a novel structured-adaptive merging approach that adapts based on adapter structure rather than using simple arithmetic addition. The authors claim their method outperforms existing cross-lingual transfer approaches across all settings.

**Strengths:**
- The conceptual decomposition of task and language abilities is intuitive and well-motivated for cross-lingual transfer.
- The proposed structured-adaptive merging method is a concrete technical contribution that moves beyond standard arithmetic merging.
- The paper addresses a practically important problem: parameter-efficient cross-lingual transfer with LLMs.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides no empirical details: no datasets, baselines, or quantitative results are reported, making it impossible to assess the actual performance gains or experimental rigor.
- The mechanism of the structured-adaptive merging is not explained in the abstract; it is unclear how the merging process "adapts based on the structure of adapters" or what makes it fundamentally different from existing methods.
- The choice of "reference task" for obtaining language ability is not justified or clarified—how is it selected, and why does this approach work better than alternatives?
- No ablation studies or analysis of the decomposition assumption are mentioned, leaving open whether the task/language ability separation is empirically validated or merely a useful framing.

**Questions:**
- What specific datasets and languages were used for evaluation, and how do the quantitative improvements compare to baselines?
- How sensitive is the method to the choice of reference task?
- Can you provide ablation studies isolating the contribution of structured-adaptive merging versus the task/language decomposition?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper presents an interesting conceptual contribution and a reasonable technical idea, but the abstract lacks sufficient empirical validation and methodological clarity to support acceptance at a top venue. Without concrete results, baselines, and a clear explanation of the structured-adaptive merging mechanism, it is difficult to assess whether the claimed improvements are substantial or whether the method is sound. The work would benefit from detailed experimental results and clearer exposition of the technical approach before resubmission.
