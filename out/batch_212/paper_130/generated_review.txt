**Review:**

This paper proposes FPTQ, a post-training quantization method for large language models that targets the W4A8 recipe (4-bit weights, 8-bit activations). The authors argue that W4A8 combines the I/O benefits of 4-bit weight quantization with the computational efficiency of 8-bit matrix operations, but requires careful handling to avoid performance degradation. The core technical contribution is a combination of layerwise activation quantization strategies and fine-grained weight quantization, featuring a novel logarithmic equalization approach for difficult layers. The method is evaluated on BLOOM, LLaMA, and LLaMA-2 across standard benchmarks, reportedly achieving state-of-the-art W4A8 performance without requiring additional fine-tuning.

**Strengths:**
- The W4A8 recipe is a practical and well-motivated target that balances compression and computational efficiency, addressing a real deployment need.
- The paper claims to achieve state-of-the-art results on multiple popular open-source LLMs (BLOOM, LLaMA, LLaMA-2) without fine-tuning, which would be valuable for practitioners.
- The proposed logarithmic equalization for activation quantization in difficult layers is a concrete technical contribution that appears to address a known challenge in quantization.
- The focus on post-training quantization (no retraining required) increases practical applicability.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides insufficient technical detail to assess the novelty and soundness of the approach. The "logarithmic equalization" is mentioned but not explained; it is unclear how it differs from or improves upon existing activation quantization methods.
- No comparison with related post-training quantization work is mentioned (e.g., other W4A8 or mixed-precision quantization methods). The claim of "state-of-the-art" cannot be evaluated without explicit baselines.
- The paper lacks clarity on what "fine-grained weight quantization" entails. Is this per-channel, per-token, or per-group quantization? How does it differ from standard approaches?
- Critical experimental details are absent from the abstract: which benchmarks are used? What are the actual performance numbers? How much performance degradation occurs compared to full precision, and how does it compare to W8A8 and W4A16?

**Questions:**
- Can the authors provide a detailed explanation of the logarithmic equalization method and its theoretical or empirical justification?
- What specific baselines (W8A8, W4A16, other W4A8 methods) are compared, and what are the quantitative results?
- How sensitive is the method to hyperparameter choices in the layerwise activation quantization?
- Does the method require any calibration data, and if so, how much?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a practical solution, but the abstract lacks sufficient technical depth and experimental validation to assess the true contribution. The core ideas appear sound, but without detailed methodology, baselines, and quantitative results, it is difficult to judge novelty or impact. A full paper with clear technical exposition and comprehensive experiments would be needed to support the claims.
