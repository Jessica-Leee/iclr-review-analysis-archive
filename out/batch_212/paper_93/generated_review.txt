**Review:**

This paper proposes DSparsE, a sparse embedding model for knowledge graph completion that replaces dense layers with adaptive sparse ones. The method incorporates a Mixture of Experts (MoE) structure at the encoding stage and residual connections at the decoding stage, aiming to improve upon existing approaches like ComDensE and InteractE. The authors report improvements of 2.3% and 3.0% in hits@1 on FB15k-237 compared to these baselines.

**Strengths:**
- The motivation is clear: addressing known limitations of existing methods (overfitting in ComDensE, feature interaction and interpretability issues in InteractE) through sparse learning is a reasonable direction.
- The combination of sparse layers with MoE and residual structures is a sensible architectural choice that could reduce parameters while maintaining expressiveness.
- Empirical results show improvements on at least one benchmark (FB15k-237) over two representative baselines.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides insufficient technical detail to assess the novelty and soundness of the approach. How exactly are sparse layers made "adaptable"? What is the precise form of the MoE component and how does it differ from standard MoE? These details are essential for reproducibility and evaluation.
- Evaluation is limited and incomplete. Results are reported only on FB15k-237 for the main comparison; performance on WN18RR is mentioned but not detailed. No ablation studies are described to validate the contribution of individual components (sparse layers, MoE, residual structure). Without ablations, it is unclear which design choices drive improvements.
- The improvements, while positive, are modest (2–3% on a single metric). No statistical significance testing, confidence intervals, or multiple runs are mentioned. The paper lacks analysis of computational efficiency gains, which is a key motivation for sparse methods.
- Missing comparisons with other recent sparse or parameter-efficient methods for knowledge graph completion, and no discussion of how DSparsE relates to broader sparse learning literature.

**Questions:**
- How are the sparse masks learned during training? Is there a sparsity regularizer, and if so, what is its form?
- What are the actual parameter counts and inference times compared to ComDensE and InteractE?
- Why are results on WN18RR not reported in the abstract? Do improvements hold on both benchmarks?
- Have ablation studies been conducted to isolate the contributions of sparse layers, MoE, and residual connections?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. While the paper addresses a relevant problem and shows empirical improvements, the abstract lacks sufficient technical clarity to evaluate the method rigorously, the experimental validation is incomplete (limited benchmarks, no ablations, no significance testing), and the gains are modest. The work would benefit from more detailed methodology, comprehensive experiments, and deeper analysis before publication at a top venue.
