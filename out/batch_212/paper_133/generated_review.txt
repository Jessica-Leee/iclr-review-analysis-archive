**Review:**

This paper proposes a Mixture of Experts (MoE) policy representation for learning diverse skills in reinforcement learning. The key innovation is combining a non-linear MoE architecture—where each expert encodes a contextual motion primitive—with a maximum entropy objective and per-expert context distributions modeled as energy-based models (EBMs). The per-expert distributions allow each expert to specialize on a context sub-space while capturing multi-modality and hard discontinuities without requiring environmental pre-knowledge. The authors claim their approach can learn precise and diverse skills on challenging robot simulation tasks.

**Strengths:**
1. **Well-motivated problem**: The paper addresses a genuine limitation of Gaussian-parameterized RL policies and provides clear motivation for learning diverse, multi-modal skills (e.g., robustness to environmental changes).
2. **Principled technical approach**: Using energy-based models for per-expert context distributions is a thoughtful design choice that avoids strong distributional assumptions and environmental pre-knowledge requirements.
3. **Integrated framework**: The combination of MoE, maximum entropy objectives, and EBM-based context distributions into a unified policy learning framework is coherent and well-articulated.

**Weaknesses:**
1. **Lack of empirical validation**: The abstract provides no experimental results, comparisons to baselines, or quantitative evidence that the approach actually learns diverse skills or outperforms alternatives. Claims about "challenging robot simulation tasks" are unsupported.
2. **Missing implementation details**: Critical details are absent—how are EBMs trained efficiently during policy gradient updates? How is expert selection differentiable? What is the computational overhead of the MoE + EBM architecture?
3. **Unclear novelty boundaries**: While the combination is novel, the individual components (MoE in RL, maximum entropy RL, EBMs) are established. The paper does not clearly articulate what is fundamentally new beyond their integration.
4. **No discussion of related work**: The abstract lacks any reference to prior work on multi-modal RL, skill learning, or mixture-of-experts approaches in RL, making it difficult to assess novelty and positioning.

**Questions:**
- How does the computational cost of training EBMs scale with the number of experts and context dimensionality?
- Can you provide quantitative results (success rates, skill diversity metrics) on standard benchmarks?
- How does the approach compare to simpler alternatives, such as learning a discrete skill selector with standard Gaussian experts?
- What happens when the true context distribution is unimodal—does the EBM-based approach still work well?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper presents an interesting idea with reasonable motivation, but the complete absence of experimental validation, empirical results, or comparisons to baselines is a critical gap for a venue like ICLR. The technical contribution, while coherent, is primarily a combination of existing techniques without sufficient evidence that the combination is effective or necessary. A full paper with comprehensive experiments would be needed to assess the true merit of this approach.
