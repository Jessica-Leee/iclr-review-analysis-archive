**Review:**

This paper proposes an in-context learning approach that guides LLMs to debug code using a "print debugging" strategy—inserting print statements to trace execution and analyse logs for bug fixing. The authors evaluate their methodology on a Leetcode problem dataset using GPT-4, reporting improvements over rubber duck debugging of 1.5% on easy problems and 17.9% on medium-level problems.

**Strengths:**
- The core idea of leveraging print debugging as an in-context learning signal is intuitive and practical, addressing a real limitation of LLMs in tackling problems with complex data structures and algorithms.
- Evaluation on Leetcode with an online judging system provides objective, reproducible metrics rather than relying on synthetic or manual evaluation.
- The comparison against a baseline (rubber duck debugging) offers some empirical grounding for the approach.

**Weaknesses:**
- The paper lacks sufficient methodological detail. The abstract does not explain how the print debugging strategy is concretely instantiated in prompts, what the exact in-context learning setup is, or how logs are analysed to guide fixes. Without this, reproducibility and understanding of the contribution are severely limited.
- The evaluation is narrow in scope: only GPT-4 is tested, only Leetcode problems are used, and no comparison to other debugging or code-generation techniques (e.g., test-driven prompting, chain-of-thought, or other structured reasoning approaches) is provided. The improvements, while notable on medium problems, lack context—how do they compare to state-of-the-art code generation methods?
- The paper does not discuss failure cases, limitations of the approach, or when print debugging is less effective. Hard-level Leetcode problems are not reported, raising questions about scalability.
- Missing critical details: dataset size, number of trials, statistical significance testing, and ablation studies to isolate the contribution of print debugging versus other factors (e.g., prompt engineering).

**Questions:**
- How exactly are print statements inserted and logs analysed in the prompting strategy? Can you provide example prompts?
- Why is rubber duck debugging chosen as the sole baseline? How does print debugging compare to other structured debugging or reasoning techniques?
- What is the performance on hard-level Leetcode problems, and why are they omitted?
- How sensitive is the approach to the specific formulation of the print debugging prompt?

**Overall score:**
3: Reject, not good enough. While the core idea has merit, the paper is severely underdeveloped. The abstract provides insufficient methodological detail to understand or reproduce the work, the evaluation is too narrow (single model, single dataset, weak baselines), and critical experimental details are missing. The work reads more as an early-stage idea than a complete contribution ready for a top-tier venue.
