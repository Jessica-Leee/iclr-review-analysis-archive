**Review:**

This paper proposes Class-Informed VAE (CI-VAE), a variant of the Variational Autoencoder that combines the standard VAE architecture with a linear discriminator layer on the latent space to enforce linear separability between classes. The goal is to enable robust latent-space linear traversal and interpolation between arbitrary pairs of observations from the same class, with applications to data augmentation and understanding biological processes such as cancer development. The authors demonstrate the method on MNIST and a colon cancer single-cell genomics dataset, showing improvements in class-specific linear traversal compared to standard VAE while maintaining comparable reconstruction error.

**Strengths:**
- The core idea is intuitive and well-motivated: adding a linear discriminator to the latent space to encourage class-separable representations is a straightforward way to enable class-specific interpolation.
- The paper addresses a practical problem with potential applications in biology and medicine, particularly for understanding disease progression.
- Evaluation on both a standard benchmark (MNIST) and a real biological dataset (colon cancer genomics) demonstrates some breadth of applicability.

**Weaknesses:**
- The technical novelty is limited. Adding a discriminator to VAE latent spaces is not new; the contribution amounts to a relatively straightforward combination of existing techniques (VAE + linear discriminator). The paper does not clearly articulate what is novel beyond this combination.
- The evaluation is insufficient. MNIST is too simple to validate the method's effectiveness; no quantitative metrics are provided for class-specific interpolation quality (e.g., classification accuracy of interpolated samples, perceptual quality measures). For the genomics application, the paper provides no quantitative validation—only a qualitative claim that interpolation "may enhance understanding," with no evidence presented.
- Missing important experimental details and comparisons. The paper does not compare against other class-aware VAE variants or conditional VAE methods (e.g., CVAE), which are natural baselines. No ablation studies are provided to justify the design choices.
- The presentation lacks rigor. The abstract and introduction do not clearly define what "class-specific linear traversal" means mathematically, nor do they explain why linear separability in latent space guarantees meaningful interpolation. The connection between the discriminator loss and the interpolation objective is not formally established.

**Questions:**
- How does CI-VAE compare quantitatively to conditional VAE (CVAE) or other class-aware generative models on MNIST and the genomics dataset?
- What quantitative metrics validate that interpolated samples remain within the same class? Are there classification or biological validity measures?
- Why is a linear discriminator sufficient? Have the authors considered nonlinear discriminators or other regularization schemes?
- For the genomics application, what biological evidence supports that the interpolated cells represent realistic intermediate states in cancer development?

**Overall score:**
4: below the acceptance threshold. While the motivation is reasonable, the technical contribution is incremental, the experimental validation is weak (especially for the main application), and the paper lacks comparison to relevant baselines. The work would benefit from stronger quantitative evaluation, clearer technical exposition, and comparison to conditional generative models.
