**Review:**

This paper proposes ElliDock, a learning-based method for rigid protein-protein docking that predicts elliptic paraboloid interfaces for each input protein and aligns them to obtain the roto-translation transformation. The key contribution is the use of elliptic paraboloid surface representations combined with an equivariant architecture that is independently equivariant with respect to arbitrary rotations and translations of the input proteins, ensuring generalization across different protein orientations. The authors report faster inference time and improved performance over state-of-the-art learning-based methods (DiffDock-PP, AlphaFold-Multimer) on antibody-antigen docking tasks.

**Strengths:**
- The elliptic paraboloid interface representation is a geometrically motivated and interpretable choice for capturing protein-protein interaction surfaces, offering a principled alternative to voxel or point-cloud representations.
- Independent equivariance with respect to rotations and translations is a well-motivated inductive bias for the docking problem, addressing a fundamental generalization requirement.
- Reported computational efficiency (fastest inference time) is a practical advantage for real-world applications in drug design and protein engineering.
- The focus on antibody-antigen docking is a well-defined and important benchmark where learning-based methods can provide value.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides limited technical detail about how the elliptic paraboloid is parameterized, predicted, and optimized. The mechanism for "making two interfaces coincide" to obtain the transformation is not clearly explained.
- No quantitative results, benchmark datasets, or numerical comparisons are provided in the abstract. Claims of outperforming DiffDock-PP and AlphaFold-Multimer lack supporting metrics (e.g., RMSD, success rates, runtime).
- The scope appears limited to antibody-antigen docking; generalization to other protein-protein docking scenarios is not discussed.
- The paper lacks discussion of how the method handles proteins with complex, non-convex interfaces or multiple potential docking modes, which are common challenges in rigid docking.

**Questions:**
- How is the elliptic paraboloid parameterized (e.g., center, axes, curvature parameters), and how many parameters does the model predict per protein?
- What is the optimization objective for aligning the two predicted interfaces, and how is the final transformation extracted?
- Can you provide quantitative results (RMSD, success rate, runtime) on standard benchmarks such as DIPS or other protein-protein docking datasets?
- How does ElliDock perform on general protein-protein docking beyond antibody-antigen complexes?

**Overall score:**
6: marginally above the acceptance threshold

The paper presents a novel geometric approach to protein docking with a principled equivariance property and demonstrates practical efficiency gains. However, the abstract lacks sufficient technical and empirical detail to fully assess the contribution. The core idea is sound and the focus on equivariance is appropriate, but the limited scope (antibody-antigen only) and absence of quantitative results in the abstract weaken the presentation. With a full paper providing detailed methodology, comprehensive benchmarks, and ablation studies, this could be a solid contribution to the field.
