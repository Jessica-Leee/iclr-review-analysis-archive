**Review:**

This paper addresses targeted generation in diffusion models by formulating the fine-tuning of the inference phase as a sequential black-box optimization problem. The authors propose a stochastic adaptive sequential optimization algorithm designed to optimize cumulative black-box scores under unknown transition dynamics, and provide a theoretical convergence rate of O(d²/√T) for cumulative convex functions without requiring smoothness or strong convexity assumptions. The method is applied to diffusion-based image generation conditioned on user-preferred target scores.

**Strengths:**

1. **Well-motivated problem formulation**: Casting diffusion model fine-tuning as sequential black-box optimization is a natural and practical approach for user-preferred targeted generation, addressing a relevant challenge in generative modeling.

2. **Theoretical contribution**: The O(d²/√T) convergence rate for cumulative convex functions without smoothness or strong convexity is a non-trivial theoretical result that extends beyond standard assumptions in optimization.

3. **Practical applicability**: The method naturally applies to diffusion-based generation tasks and demonstrates empirical ability to generate target-guided images with high target scores.

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Limited experimental validation**: The abstract provides no details on experimental setup, baselines, datasets, or quantitative comparisons. It is unclear how the proposed method compares against existing approaches for guided diffusion generation (e.g., classifier-free guidance, gradient-based methods) or other black-box optimization techniques.

2. **Unclear algorithmic novelty**: The abstract does not sufficiently explain what makes the proposed "stochastic adaptive sequential optimization algorithm" novel compared to existing sequential or bandit-based optimization methods. The connection between the theoretical algorithm and its practical instantiation for diffusion models is not articulated.

3. **Gap between theory and practice**: The theoretical analysis assumes cumulative convex functions and unknown transition dynamics, but it is unclear how these assumptions map to the diffusion generation setting. The paper does not discuss whether the diffusion objective satisfies convexity or how the theory justifies the empirical algorithm.

4. **Incomplete presentation**: The abstract lacks essential details on the method's hyperparameters, computational cost, scalability, and failure modes. No discussion of limitations or when the approach might underperform is provided.

**Questions:**

- How does the proposed method compare quantitatively to existing guided diffusion approaches (e.g., classifier guidance, DDIM with gradient-based steering) on standard benchmarks?
- What is the computational overhead of the sequential optimization procedure relative to standard diffusion sampling?
- Can you provide ablation studies showing the contribution of the "adaptive" and "stochastic" components?
- How sensitive is the method to the choice of target score function, and does it work with non-convex or noisy target scores in practice?

**Overall score:**

5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper tackles a relevant problem and claims both theoretical and empirical contributions. However, the abstract is too sparse on experimental details, baseline comparisons, and algorithmic clarity to assess the true novelty and impact. The gap between the theoretical assumptions (cumulative convex functions) and the practical setting (diffusion generation) is not adequately bridged. Stronger empirical validation with detailed comparisons and clearer exposition of the algorithmic innovation would be needed to meet the acceptance bar.
