**Review:**

This paper addresses long-term typhoon trajectory prediction by proposing a physics-conditioned approach that leverages real-time Unified Model (UM) data instead of reanalysis data like ERA5. The authors claim their method provides 6-hourly predictions up to 72 hours in advance and outperforms both state-of-the-art data-driven methods and numerical weather prediction models. They also contribute the PHYSICS TRACK dataset, which combines ERA5 reanalysis, typhoon best-track observations, and UM forecast data.

**Strengths:** (1) The motivation is well-articulated: the paper identifies a genuine practical limitation—reanalysis data is not produced in real-time and lags behind operational needs—and proposes using real-time UM forecasts as an alternative. (2) The release of a preprocessed dataset combining multiple data sources (ERA5, best-track, UM) could be valuable for the community. (3) The problem is timely and socially relevant, with clear applications to disaster preparedness.

**Weaknesses:** (1) The abstract provides almost no technical detail about the proposed method itself. What is the "physics-conditioned approach"? Is it a neural network, a hybrid model, a loss function modification? How exactly does it incorporate physics? Without this information, it is impossible to assess novelty or soundness. (2) No experimental results, baselines, or quantitative comparisons are presented in the abstract. Claims that the method "outperforms both state-of-the-art data-driven methods and numerical weather prediction models" are unsupported by any metrics, datasets, or ablations. (3) The distinction between using UM forecast data versus reanalysis data is presented as a major contribution, but the abstract does not explain why this choice fundamentally changes the approach or what architectural/methodological innovations follow from it. (4) Critical details are missing: What is the model architecture? How is physics conditioning implemented? What are the evaluation metrics and baselines? How large is the dataset? These omissions make it impossible to assess the paper's technical contribution.

**Assessment:** While the problem is important and the dataset contribution is potentially useful, the abstract reads more like a position paper or dataset announcement than a research contribution with a novel method. The lack of any technical description of the proposed approach, combined with unsupported performance claims and no empirical evidence, falls well short of ICLR standards. The paper would need substantial additional detail on methodology, comprehensive experiments with clear baselines, and ablation studies to be suitable for publication at a top venue.
