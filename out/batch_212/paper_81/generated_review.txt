**Review:**

This paper proposes Complex-valued Scattering Representations (CSR) to improve complex-valued deep learning by incorporating spatial and spatial-frequency properties of input data. The authors construct filters based on complex-valued Morlet wavelets with tunable parameters and develop a learnable high-dimensional complex-valued ReLU activation function. The method is evaluated on RGB, multi-spectral image (MSI), and MRI patch classification tasks, with reported improvements over real-valued and existing complex-valued baselines, particularly in limited labeled data regimes.

**Strengths:**
- The motivation is clear: existing complex-valued representations focus narrowly on channel characteristics and lack spatial-frequency structure, which CSR addresses through wavelet-based filters.
- The paper targets a practically relevant problem—complex-valued networks on diverse data modalities (RGB, MSI, MRI)—and claims improvements especially under data scarcity.
- The combination of complex-valued Morlet wavelets with learnable high-dimensional complex-valued ReLU is a concrete technical contribution that extends prior work on complex-valued architectures.

**Weaknesses:**
- The abstract provides no experimental details, quantitative results, or dataset specifications, making it impossible to assess the magnitude of improvements or reproducibility. Specific accuracy numbers, baselines, and dataset sizes are essential.
- The novelty is somewhat incremental: applying Morlet wavelets (well-established in signal processing and scattering networks) to complex-valued learning, and adding a learnable ReLU variant, are natural extensions rather than fundamentally new insights.
- No discussion of computational cost, scalability, or theoretical justification for why complex-valued Morlet wavelets should outperform alternatives. The claim of "universal" representations is not substantiated.
- The abstract lacks clarity on how the method integrates into existing complex-valued networks and whether it replaces or augments standard layers. The relationship to classical scattering networks (Mallat et al.) is not explained.

**Questions:**
- What are the absolute accuracy improvements over baselines, and on how many datasets/tasks are they consistent?
- How does computational cost compare to real-valued and standard complex-valued models?
- Can you provide ablation studies isolating the contribution of wavelet filters vs. learnable complex-valued ReLU?
- How do results scale with network depth and data size beyond the "limited labeled data" setting?

**Overall score:**
5: marginally below the acceptance threshold

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a concrete method combining wavelets with learnable activations. However, the abstract alone lacks sufficient experimental validation, quantitative results, and clarity on novelty and integration. For a venue like ICLR, the contribution feels incremental without strong empirical evidence or theoretical insight. A full paper with detailed experiments, ablations, and comparisons would be needed to assess acceptance.
